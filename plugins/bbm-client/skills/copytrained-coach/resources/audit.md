This is the audit engine this package's modes follow. A domain's own audit skill (for example the sales-page section audit) tells you what to read, then points here for all judging, scoring, and report format.

Purpose: assess whether a piece of copy is good by scoring it against a doctrine, and explain in plain language what's working and what you can improve.

The audit's only job is to ASSESS. It identifies what's strong and what you can improve; it does NOT write or rewrite anything. If I want something rewritten, I'll ask separately.

## The five engine rules (apply to EVERY criterion pool, in every domain)

These govern how criteria become a band. They replace a family of per-file clauses and they are stated once, here.

### S1. Presence is a precondition, not a point

**"Has a [section]" is a PRECONDITION, not a criterion.** Inside a section being audited, its existence is not an achievement.

- **Absent** → the section is ❌ at page level, which is already handled there.
- **Present** → scoring starts at the quality criteria.

**The "clearly headlined" half survives only where headline QUALITY is actually judged.** The bare has-it half is out of every pool.

### S2. Virtue-by-absence criteria are VIOLATION CHECKS: they only pull down

A family of criteria are met by simply not sinning: honest and realistic · no hard push · believable and not too-good · soft framing · genuine rather than fake · no external links · no third-person self-reference · quoted language kept intact.

**Not-sinning is the baseline, not an achievement.** So:

- **A violation check is NEVER counted as a MET toward a band.**
- **A violation forces the band down one level**, and it is flagged in the prose.
- **★ A trust-breaker forces 🔴 outright:** fabricated-looking claims, unfair or false competitor claims.
- **★ Urgency and scarcity are never judged as genuine or fake.** Countdown timers, evergreen deadlines, expiring discounts and limited spots are the owner's choice. The urgency doctrine's "genuine, not fake" criterion is never judged and is excluded from every pool, silently. Where any criterion asks whether urgency is "real" or "genuine", judge only whether it is clearly communicated. This includes the editing layer's "Authentic, not salesy" check: a timer, a deadline, an expiring discount or limited spots never count against it. Pushy wording still does ("ACT NOW!!!", threats of regret), because that is language.

### S3. Judge once: one virtue never earns two METs

The editing layer rides with every section, and the sections' own criteria already duplicate most of it. **Copy strong on one dimension currently gets credited two or three times for it, and the pool rounds up.**

**Where a section's own criteria and the editing layer test the same dimension, judge that dimension ONCE. The section's version wins**, because it is sharper, and the editing layer's duplicate is skipped for that section, silently.

**The editing layer remains the full layer only for dimensions a section's own pool does not cover.**

### S4. Bonuses never move bands

**A bonus criterion informs the prose and never touches a band or a number.** Met, it may earn a "worth adding" line. Missing, it is silent.

**The "(bonus ...)" tags in the doctrine stay as routing:** they tell you the material is prose-only.

### S5. One job governs

**Several sections carry a named one-job criterion**, marked in their own file. It is the section's reason to exist.

- **Band from the one-job criterion FIRST.**
- **The rest of the pool adjusts the band at most ONE step from there.**
- **★ A section that fails its one job caps at 🔴**, whatever else it met.

### The min-pool rule

**A section may not band on fewer than three judged criteria.** Below that, report it qualitatively ("mostly design elements I cannot see, here is what to check") and **exclude it from the score with its one-line reason.**

**This matters most on a pasted page**, where visual-heavy sections shrink to one or two judgeable criteria and their bands become volatile and generous.

## Judging scale (be precise and consistent)
Judge every criterion against its literal wording, the same way every time. The same copy must always get the same judgment — inconsistency is not acceptable.
- **MET** — every element the criterion asks for is actually present and clear. If a criterion names a specific requirement (e.g. the exact claim step, the specific contact point, a specific number), that element must really be there. Do not round up.
- **PARTIAL (counts as half)** — the idea is present but an element the criterion asks for is missing, vague, or weak. Example: a refund path that says "email me" or "contact me" but never gives the actual address or exact step is PARTIAL, not MET.
- **MISSING** — the criterion is essentially absent (e.g. no claim instructions at all).
- **NOT-RELEVANT** — excluded per the rules (user-marked, or a clearly-inapplicable product-dependent criterion).
- When torn between two levels, choose the LOWER one. A criterion is only MET when nothing it asks for is absent.

## Scoring

Two layers, and they do different jobs. **The per-section band is the diagnostic layer. The Copy Score is the headline number, and it is a component score.**

### The type layer

- **A sales page is scored exactly as written below.** Its rows and weights are fixed.
- **Every other type takes its rows and weights from its entry in `copy-types.md`:** The Argument (its points), the Conversion Assets rows (each with its points), Craft (its points). The three always sum to 100.
- **The Argument for any type** is the band-average over the parts that carry the piece's argument (the entry's Argument row says what that is), multiplied by the type's Argument points. A short piece with one argument part bands that part.
- **Craft for any type** is the craft layer judged across the piece, as below, scaled to the type's Craft points, and judged against the piece's own job (tightness and hook points on a 90-word ad are not judged against a 4,000-word page).
- **Conversion Assets for any type** follow the same MET / PARTIAL / MISSING discipline as below; **★ nothing inside a type's own rows can be set aside**, and an absent element scores zero.
- **The drop-and-rescale arithmetic** (`copy-types.md`'s rule 4): when a row is dropped, each remaining Conversion Assets row's maximum becomes its points × (the component's points ÷ the sum of the remaining rows' points), rounded to whole points, with the largest row absorbing any rounding difference so the component total is unchanged. The scorecard shows the rescaled maximums. **The sales-page entry never drops a row.**
- **A funnel or a sequence audited as one piece** uses the "Funnels and sequences, audited as one piece" entry; its "each step does its own job" row is the average of the steps' band points × that row's points.
- **A hosted checkout or order bump** follows `copy-types.md`'s block on hosted platforms: a lever the platform does not allow is dropped and the rest rescale.

### Layer 1: the per-section band (the diagnostic layer, retained)

**Band each section from its combined criteria pool** (its own criteria plus the editing layer, as one pool, never counted or shown separately), **under the five engine rules above**.

- **🟢 Solid (1.0):** does the section's core job well; only minor gaps.
- **🟡 Needs work (0.6):** does the core job with real, fixable gaps.
- **🔴 Barely working (0.3):** present but missing most of what it needs, or complete but hollow.
- **❌ Missing (0.0):** absent, and it should be here.
- **➖ Set aside (excluded):** genuinely does not apply, or below the min-pool threshold. Excluded with a one-line reason.

**When torn between two bands, choose the LOWER one.** Do not let "all the parts are present" round a hollow section up.

**These bands drive the line-by-line markup and feed The Argument component below.** They are not the headline number.

**For a SINGLE-section audit there is no page, so there is no component score.** Report the section's band and its band-score (🟢 100 · 🟡 60 · 🔴 30 · ❌ 0), and say plainly that it is a picture of this section rather than a page number.

### Layer 2: the Copy Score (out of 100, three components)

**The Copy Score answers whether the page SELLS, not how much of the standard is present.** Three components, summed.

```
Copy Score  =  The Argument (0-30)  +  Conversion Assets (0-45)  +  Craft (0-25)
```

#### The Argument (0 to 30)

**The band-average over the argument sections**, computed under the five engine rules: headline, lead, twisting the knife, the reframe, the mechanisms, painting the dream, education, and the story sections.

**Average their band points** (🟢 1.0 · 🟡 0.6 · 🔴 0.3 · ❌ 0.0, set-asides excluded) **and multiply by 30.**

#### Conversion Assets (0 to 45)

**★ NOTHING in this component can be set aside.** A page without a guarantee does not get the risk-reversal points excluded; it scores zero on them. **That is the point of the component: these are the things that make a page sell, and their absence is the finding.**

| Asset | Points | Judged on |
|---|---:|---|
| **Proof** | **12** | The testimonial, results, placement, studies and personal-transformation criteria, plus the proof layer's strong/moderate/weak scale. **Placement is part of the quality bar**, not just presence |
| **Objections** | **8** | The objection-handling criteria, the FAQ's product-specific and answer-craft criteria, and the objection-answering criteria in the subheadline and lead |
| **Risk reversal** | **8** | The guarantee's terms, claim-step and sells-safety criteria. **Honest equivalents are credited**: a trial, an audit-first offer, a real refund practice stated plainly |
| **The promise** | **6** | The headline's one job and its matched promise-and-proof pair, painting the dream's core criterion, and the offer introduction's plain-language test |
| **Offer clarity and the price case** | **6** | The offer introduction's clarity criteria, the price-justification criteria, and the buy block's summary criterion |
| **Reason to act now** | **5** | The urgency criteria, the close's decision prompt, and market timing. Judged on how clearly the urgency is communicated; whether it is genuine is never judged (S2). |

**Score each asset on its own points**, using the same MET / PARTIAL / MISSING discipline: full points where the asset is present and genuinely working, roughly half where it is present and thin, zero where it is absent or hollow.

#### Craft (0 to 25)

**The craft-layer criteria judged across the whole page**, under S3's judge-once resolution: clarity, tone, the audience's language, showing rather than telling, powerful language, tightness, emotion, flow, hook points, subheadings.

**Where a section's own criteria already judged a dimension, it is not judged again here.**

### The distribution anchors

**These are where the numbers should land.** Read them before reporting a score.

- **85+:** publish-grade, and rare.
- **70s:** good, with real gaps.
- **50s:** the typical earnest first attempt.
- **30s:** a structural rebuild.

**★ Most pages land 40 to 65. If your audits routinely land above 70, you are rounding up.**

### The meaning line

**State in one line what the number is.** It reflects how well the page sells, judged from the copy you were given. **The Coach judges copy**, so anything that lives only in the design is excluded and said so.

**If the user marks something not-relevant, recompute and show the new number.**

## Report format
Write the WHOLE report in plain, natural prose, the way a copy coach would say it out loud. The person reading has never seen the doctrine or its checklist, so the feedback must be fully self-explanatory. NEVER reference criterion numbers or internal labels (no "#6", no "How to claim is clear — MET"). Say the thing in plain language instead (e.g. not "#6 How to claim is clear — MET" but "Buyers can clearly see how to claim a refund: you give both a phone number and an email").

**ONE unified report.** The editing-layer criteria and the section's own criteria appear together in the SAME prose and the SAME scorecard, woven in wherever they naturally fit. Do NOT create a separate "editing" or "craft" section, heading, or scorecard block, and never tell me a point comes from a craft layer versus the section. To me it is a single combined assessment of this copy.

**Copy Score: [X]/100 · [🟢 Solid / 🟡 Needs work / 🔴 Barely working]** (the band and number from Scoring above). The colour on the total, in every mode: 🟢 70 and up, 🟡 40 to 69, 🔴 below 40.

**★ Report the band without warming it.** No softening, no cheerleading, no "great start!" wrapped around a 🔴. The band is the finding; **an encouraging frame around a weak number is the number lying.** The coach voice is warm about the PERSON and exact about the WORK, and those are different things.

**Don't let the score speak for the copy.** The number shows how much of the standard is PRESENT, not whether the copy truly persuades. A section can tick most boxes and still be hollow: present but with weak or generic voice, no real proof, vague language, no genuine emotion. When that's the case, say so plainly and PROMINENTLY in the prose, and lead with it — and band it down. State clearly when copy is "complete but hollow." Never let a healthy-looking score imply the copy is strong when the substance isn't there.

The prose (the improvements) is the MAIN body of the audit and must be FULL and in-depth, the way an experienced copy coach would talk it through. The scorecard at the end is an ADDITION, never a reason to shorten or thin out this analysis. Go deep here.

**Full means more short paragraphs and bullets, never longer paragraphs.** Every paragraph follows `coach-voice.md`'s rule: one idea, about 50 words at most.

**Lead with what to improve, not with praise.** The body is the improvements — what's weak or missing and exactly how to strengthen it. Do NOT write a thorough "what's working" tour: name a strength only when it carries a lesson the user can use (it models a technique a weaker part should borrow, or it's the one-line context a fix needs). Bare praise that leads to no change wastes the reader's attention; cut it. This matches the improvements-only direction of the whole-page audits.

**The X improvements you can make** (title it with the actual count, e.g. "The 3 improvements you can make", or "The 1 improvement you can make" for a single point): this is a softer, on-your-side frame around the SAME direct, honest critique ("here's how to make this stronger"). Do NOT make the feedback vaguer, gentler, or hedged; the substance, depth, and honesty stay exactly the same, and you still lead with the most important issue. For each weak or missing point, in plain prose and in real depth:
- Say what's weak or missing, plainly.
- Explain WHY it weakens the copy, grounded in the doctrine and its examples (the standard, not invented opinion) — but explained in plain language, not via criterion labels. Describe an illustrating example anonymously and concretely when it sharpens the point (this explains the standard; it is not writing/rewriting the user's copy).
- **★ Read the doctrine's labelled EXAMPLES, not just its criteria, before you score.** The criteria sit near the top of each doctrine file; the examples that calibrate where the bar actually sits are further down. Judging from the criteria alone sets the bar too generously — you hand out MET/PARTIAL bands the examples would have tightened — and that is the likeliest reason a page scores far higher here than an honest read would give it. A criteria-only read produces a plausible scorecard and an inflated score.

If I tell you to mark something not-relevant, recompute the score with it excluded and show the new number.

**Scorecard** — place this LAST, after all the prose and the final score line. It's a report card: scannable at a glance, one line per scoring item, NOT a paragraph of bullets and NOT grouped by status. It COMPLEMENTS the in-depth prose above and never replaces or shortens it (keep "what's working / what you can improve" as the full main body).

**This criteria scorecard belongs to the single-section audit.** The whole-piece modes (Section Map, deep, 5 wins) show only the scorecard their own mode file describes.

Rules for the scorecard:
- List every scoring item on its own line, in the order it would naturally appear in the copy (natural reading order), NOT grouped by status.
- Each line = a status marker + the item in plain language + a few terse words of reason. Status markers: ✅ met · ⚠️ partial · ❌ missing · ➖ set aside (not scored).
- Plain language only: no criterion numbers, no doctrine terms (follow the plain-language rule in SKILL.md's standing rules).
- A met item may stand alone or carry a few words; partial/missing/set-aside items should always give the terse reason.
- End with ONE tally line showing the criteria breakdown AND the section's band/score, e.g.: `11 met · 2 partial · 3 missing · 1 set aside → 🟡 Needs work, 60/100`. The criteria breakdown is the granular detail (which specific things are present or missing); the band + Copy Score is the headline, matching Scoring above.

Format:

```
Scorecard
✅ Guarantee present
✅ Headline — leads with loving the product, not just a refund
✅ Personal tone — warm and human
✅ Clear terms — 30 days, just let them know
✅ Easy to claim — phone and email given
⚠️ [Partial item] — what's half-there
❌ Trust badge — none on the page
❌ Visibility — sits low, buyers scroll past it
➖ Proof the product works — set aside, doesn't apply to a taste-based product
5 met · 1 partial · 2 missing · 1 set aside → 🟡 Needs work, 60/100
```

## Rules
- Follow the global plain-language rule in SKILL.md's standing rules: never name internal doctrine terms or examples to the user (no "scroll test," "Astra AI example," "criterion #N," "the doctrine says"). Explain every concept in plain words, and when illustrating with an example, describe it anonymously and concretely rather than by name.
- Follow the global product-dependent-criteria rule in SKILL.md's standing rules: for criteria tagged "(product-dependent)," use common sense. Judge if it clearly applies, set aside and tell the user if it clearly doesn't; genuinely ambiguous: make the best call and say it in one line; the user can override and the score is recomputed. Never set aside a universal (untagged) criterion.
- The score must be honest and reflect ONLY applicable (not not-relevant) criteria.
- Reasoning comes from the doctrine/examples, never invented opinion.
- The report assesses; it does not rewrite the copy. Offers live only in SKILL.md's close. (I can still choose to mark something not-relevant.)
- Never fabricate human material (stories, proof, specifics).
- One final score only — never provisional-then-final.

**Shared set-aside / mapping rules (these match the whole-page audits — apply them so a section scored here lands the same way it would in a full-page audit):**
- **Don't call a scaffolded-but-empty section "missing."** If the copy sets a section up (a heading + lead-in) but its content isn't in the text you were given, assume it's an image or embed you couldn't read, not absent content (a bonus stack, a testimonial gallery, an expert endorsement, a comparison graphic). Name it in the "couldn't read" line (SKILL.md); never band it 🔴 and never count it as missing. Only call something a genuine gap when the copy neither contains it nor scaffolds it anywhere.
- **A quote headline usually means a story lead.** When the headline itself is a quote in the reader's or a customer's voice (rather than a statement), a missing eyebrow or a separate subheadline is usually the right call — don't treat it as a gap and don't suggest adding one.
- **The repeated buy/pricing block is correct by design.** Per `buy-buttons.md`, the buy/pricing block is meant to appear once or twice, and the copies are meant to be identical. If you're auditing a buy section (or the full page) and see the pricing block repeat, never flag it as duplication or suggest shortening the second copy. (Whole-page context; applies when the repeat is actually in view.)
