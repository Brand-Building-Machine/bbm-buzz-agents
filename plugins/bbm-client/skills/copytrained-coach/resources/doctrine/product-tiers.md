# Product Tiers — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- Offering tiers, rather than a single option, does two things: it converts clients who want more than the base product (extra support or done-for-you work), and it raises your average order value. It is close to a must-have when you have tiers to offer.
- Higher tiers (often called VIP, Pro, or Master) can include additional content, tools, live or small-group coaching, 1-on-1 calls, critiques, audits, personalized plans, or done-for-you work.

### The section
- If you offer higher tiers, add a section explaining what each additional tier includes, ideally with tier-specific testimonials. Place it right before the buy button (before or after the guarantee), or after the What You'll Learn section.
- For each tier: a headline naming the tier, what it includes and how it works, the benefits (why upgrade), who it is perfect for, and proof (ideally testimonials specific to that tier).
- For a substantial premium tier, you can model the How it Works, What You'll Learn, or Feature Deep Dives structure to give buyers enough to choose the right tier.
- This section applies only to products with multiple tiers. For a single-tier product, set it aside.

## Examples

- **A clean tier framework (Body Mechanics Mastery, Recorded Video Critiques):** a headline with the feature and benefit ("level up 2x as fast"), a reiteration of the base program's value, a reason to consider the tier ("most animators improve 2x faster with a veteran critiquing them"), extra benefits, how the two critique options work, personal proof, and a testimonial. — why it works: it follows a clean framework (headline, reason to upgrade, benefits, how it works, proof) and ends on a tier-specific testimonial.

- **Making the tier's value simple (Recursion, Pro-only bonus):** the headline clarifies it is Pro-only, opens with reasons to care (weaker alternatives like asking a friend or hiring a coach), the benefit (stop wasting time and money), and what the Pro tier includes. — why it works: it makes the Pro-only nature and its value clear and simple.

- **Modeling What You'll Learn for a big tier (Webflow Masterclass, the "Pro" Track):** a headline naming the tier and its benefits (scale your business, premium clients), the extra content list, how the tier works, plus a separate "What's Inside the Pro Track" section modeled on a What You'll Learn. — why it works: for a substantial premium tier, it borrows the What You'll Learn structure so buyers have enough to choose the right tier.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Has a section describing each additional tier:** Is there a section describing each additional tier the product offers? MET when each tier is clearly described. PARTIAL when tiers are listed but barely explained. Set aside entirely for single-tier products (there are no tiers to describe). (product-dependent)
2. **Each tier is fully sold: name, contents, and who it is for:** Per tier, is there a clear name, what it actually includes, and the buyer it is meant for? MET when all three hold for every tier. PARTIAL when the contents are clear but the who-it-is-for is missing. MISSING when the tiers are price points with a list under them.
3. **The upgrade case is made:** Does the copy give a real reason to choose the higher tier, rather than leaving the reader to work it out from a feature table? MET when the extra value is argued and tied to a buyer's situation. PARTIAL when the difference is listed but never argued. MISSING when the tiers are presented with no case for moving up.
6. **Provides proof for each tier:** Does it provide proof for each tier, ideally tier-specific testimonials? MET when there is tier-specific proof. PARTIAL when the proof is generic (not tier-specific). Set aside if no tier-specific proof exists yet; never fabricate; where the proof is a screenshot, confirm it with the user. (product-dependent)
