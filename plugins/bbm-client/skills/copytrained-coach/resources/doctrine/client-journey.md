# Client Journey — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- Right after the Offer Introduction, you can add a visual roadmap of how the program works, to get clients excited about the progress they will make and to grab attention.
- This is an optional section (a page does not need one). If you have the design skills and it sparks ideas, add it. Align it with your "How it Works" section.

### What it includes
- A graphic that clearly shows the progression the client moves through, stage by stage.
- Named stages or modules.
- A key benefit or milestone attached to each stage.
- Specific time frames where they fit (some journeys are time-based, like "your next 6 months"; others are level-based, like Level 1 to Level 7).

## Examples

- **Time-based stages toward the end result (Employee to Entrepreneur):** an upward chart labeled "Your next 6 months," moving through Maximize Impact, Maximize Income, Maximize Speed, and Maximize Freedom ("Goodbye 9-5"), each with a step. — why it works: it maps the exact stages the client moves through toward the end result (leaving the 9-5), with a step at each stage and a clear time frame.

- **Gamified named levels (Next Level Playing):** a staircase of seven named levels, from Level 1 (Fretboard Fundamentals) up to Level 7 (Mixolydian Mastery). — why it works: a simple, gamified staircase of named levels that makes progress feel exciting and concrete.

- **Named levels with described results (Notion Mastery, "The Student Journey"):** Level 0 (Hello Notion), Level 1 (Notion Essentials), Level 2 (Notion Dashboards), Level 3 (Notion Zen Master), each with a description of what the client builds. — why it works: it names each level and describes the results at each, walking the reader through exactly what they will build.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Shows a clear, ordered progression:** Does it show a clear, ordered progression of the stages the client moves through? MET when the stages are clear and in order. PARTIAL when the progression is muddled. MISSING when there is no real progression.
2. **Names the stages or modules:** Are the stages or modules clearly named? MET when each stage is named. PARTIAL when only some are. MISSING when the stages are unnamed.
3. **Attaches a benefit or milestone to each stage:** Does each stage carry a key benefit or milestone? MET when each stage has one. PARTIAL when only some do. MISSING when the stages are just labels with no payoff.
4. **Includes specific time frames:** Does it include specific time frames where they fit ("your next 6 months")? (bonus — if met it can raise the score; being missing does NOT count against the score, since many level-based journeys have no time frames)
5. **Presented as an engaging, clear graphic:** Is it presented as an engaging, clear graphic that makes the progress feel exciting? (visual — audit must ask the user)
