# Video Testimonials — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- In addition to written testimonials, include a handful of video testimonials. They are more engaging and add emotion and depth, and even when they go unwatched, their presence adds trust and credibility. This is strongly recommended; where you do not have them yet, collecting them is a high strategic priority.

### How to make them strong
- Formats: embed several side-by-side, a hybrid of a text quote plus a video, or a montage of many testimonials in one video.
- Show, don't tell: edit in specific results where possible (the client demonstrating the skill or result on camera, a before/after of their website traffic).
- Use attention-grabbing thumbnails, ideally with a text hook, so people actually watch.
- Add captions or subtitles: many people watch without audio, captions raise watch rates, and they help with accents or poor audio.

### Audit dependency and integrity
- Video testimonials are videos, so ask the user about the thumbnails, captions, results shown, and whether there is a montage, then score those. Judge the framing headline above the videos directly. Never fabricate a video testimonial.

## Examples

- **Embedded side-by-side, one showing results (a creator page):** several embedded videos with subscriber counts, one of which shows a specific result on camera. — why it works: embedding several videos adds trust, and a video that shows a real result is proof, not just praise.

- **A hybrid text-plus-video, showing the skill (Padel School):** a text quote ("I've won my first tournament") paired with a video of the client actually playing padel. — why it works: showing the client doing the thing (playing padel) is show-don't-tell proof of the outcome.

- **Thumbnails with hooks (Jumpspeak):** video thumbnails with text-hook overlays ("Intermediate to Advanced," "I stopped feeling embarrassed completely"). — why it works: a hook on the thumbnail gets far more people to actually watch.

- **A montage (Learn Practice Play, BASB):** a grid or single montage of many student videos. — why it works: a montage packs many testimonials into one engaging block that adds credibility at a glance.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Has video testimonials:** Are there video testimonials (aim for at least 5)? (visual — audit must ask the user) (Absence is a high-priority gap to fill, not a shrug-off.)
2. **Attention-grabbing thumbnails:** Do the videos have attention-grabbing thumbnails, ideally with a text hook? (visual — audit must ask the user)
3. **Captions or subtitles:** Do the videos have captions or subtitles? (visual — audit must ask the user)
4. **An attention-grabbing headline above the videos:** Is there a compelling headline framing the video testimonials ("Proof it works," "See what our clients are saying")? MET when the framing headline is compelling. PARTIAL when generic. MISSING when there is none. (This framing copy is judgeable directly.)
5. **Show, don't tell (results edited in):** Do the videos edit in specific results where possible (the client demonstrating the skill or result on camera)? (audit must ask the user)
6. **Includes a montage:** Is there a video testimonial montage? (visual — audit must ask the user) (bonus — a nice addition; absence does NOT count against the score)
