# Before and Afters — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- Before-and-afters are one of the strongest proof tools. They show that you take clients from point A (their current pain, the thing your copy addresses) to point B (their desired outcome). This is strongly recommended, and where you do not have them yet, collecting them is a high strategic priority.
- Fitness and supplement products should absolutely have them. And almost any industry can, with planning: animation before/after shots, a public-speaking before/after speech, an SEO before/after traffic graph, a home-studio before/after, a tennis-swing before/after.

### How to build them
- Collect them by capturing the "before" state up front (a welcome survey, with contact details and visuals) and the "after" later (a check-in or feedback survey, or a Zoom interview). Then write before/after testimonials or case studies from the data.
- Formats: side-by-side photos, an interactive drag slider, a before/after grid, or split-screen videos.
- Pair each before/after with a strong headline or a specific result.

### Audit dependency and integrity
- Before-and-afters are visual, so ask the user what before/afters they have (how many, what they show, and the headlines or results attached), then score. Never fabricate a before/after.

## Examples

- **Fitness and supplements, with numbers (MacroFactor, BODi, Momentous):** side-by-side transformation photos and results ("lost 26 lbs in 80 days"), and a supplement before/after (insomnia to restful sleep). — why it works: for fitness and supplements, before/afters are a must, and pairing them with specific numbers makes them stronger.

- **An interactive slider with annotated changes (Super Spicy Media):** a drag slider between a "before" social feed and an "after" one, with the specific changes highlighted. — why it works: an interactive before/after with annotated changes shows exactly what improved.

- **A before/after grid at scale (Home Studio Blueprint):** a grid of student webcam before/afters. — why it works: a grid shows the transformation happening across many students, not just one.

- **Split-screen video (Effortless Forehand, Bar Muscle Up):** a split-screen video of the same person before and after (a tennis swing, a bar muscle-up). — why it works: a video before/after shows the improvement in motion, which is hard to fake and easy to feel.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Has strong visual before-and-afters:** Are there strong visual before-and-afters (aim for at least 5)? (visual — audit must ask the user) (For most products, absence is a high-priority gap to fill, not a shrug-off; only set aside if a visual before/after genuinely cannot exist for this product.)
2. **They map to the promised transformation:** Do the before/afters map to the transformation the copy promises (the "before" reflects the audience's current pain, the "after" their desired outcome)? MET when the mapping is clear. PARTIAL when it is loose. MISSING when the before/afters do not match what the copy promises.
3. **Each carries a headline or specific result:** Does each before/after carry a strong headline or a specific result? MET when they do. PARTIAL when only some do. MISSING when they are bare images with no framing.
