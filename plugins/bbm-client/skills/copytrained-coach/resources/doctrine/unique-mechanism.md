# Unique Mechanism — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- The Unique Mechanism (or methodology) is the named reason your product works. In a crowded market, especially when you promise the same outcome as competitors, you need one to stand out. It gives readers hope that a new approach will finally work for them, it makes your product hard to copy, and it positions you as superior.
- Have one even if your market is not crowded; it always makes the product more compelling and more competition-proof over time. This is a must-have section.

### The three pillars
- **Name it:** give it a unique name that does not already exist, and make it simple, memorable, clear, and curiosity-inducing.
- **Explain it:** how it works, which problems it solves, which outcome it delivers, and why it beats the alternatives.
- **Visualize it:** show a graphic (a flywheel, a Venn diagram, a numbered process, an acronym) that makes it easier to grasp and grabs attention.
- Physical products do this too (a named material like "GripForMe," a named alignment system). The strongest mechanisms check all three pillars.

## Examples

- **Named, explained, visualized (Podcast Marketing Academy, "Podcast Growth Engine"):** a named engine built on four components (Exposure, Attraction, Retention, Revenue), each explained with why it matters, shown as a circular flywheel graphic. — why it works: it names the mechanism, explains each part and the reason to care, and visualizes it so the whole system is graspable at a glance.

- **A simple, memorable acronym (BASB, "CODE"):** "CODE is our proven process for turning the information you consume into creative output," shown as a letter graphic (Capture, Organize, Distill, Express). — why it works: an acronym that is easy to remember, visualized clearly, describing the proven process behind the outcome.

- **A curiosity-inducing name and a clear visual (AI Dream Team, "The 3 Cs Prompt Framework"):** Content, Clarity, Cues, shown as a Venn diagram that overlaps into "Genius Results." — why it works: a simple, memorable name and a diagram that makes the method instantly graspable.

- **A named step-process (Fear of Falling, "The 5-Step Process to Finally Overcome Fear of Falling"):** five named steps, each explained with what it does and why it matters. — why it works: it names the methodology and walks through each step, showing exactly how the product delivers the outcome.

- **Physical-product mechanisms (Liforme Yoga Mat):** "GripForMe material" (a named material that stays grippy when sweaty) and a named "Alignment System" (numbered lines that help you position poses). — why it works: named materials and systems differentiate a physical product in a saturated market, and each is explained with the problem it solves.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Has a unique mechanism or methodology:** Is there a distinct mechanism or methodology behind why the product works, that differentiates it? MET when there is a clear, distinct mechanism. PARTIAL when it gestures at an approach without a real distinct mechanism. MISSING when there is none.
2. **It is named well:** Is the mechanism named, with a name that is unique (does not already exist), simple, memorable, clear, and curiosity-inducing? MET when the name is strong and distinctive. PARTIAL when it is named but generic or clunky. MISSING when it is unnamed.
3. **Explains how it works and why it is superior:** Does it explain how the mechanism works, which problems it solves, the outcome it delivers, and why it beats the alternatives? MET when these are clear. PARTIAL when it names the mechanism but explains it thinly. MISSING when there is no real explanation.
4. **Visualized with a graphic:** Is the mechanism visualized with a graphic (a flywheel, a Venn diagram, a numbered process, an acronym)? A strong mechanism ideally has one. (visual — audit must ask the user)
