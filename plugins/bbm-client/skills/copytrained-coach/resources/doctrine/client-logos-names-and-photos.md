# Client Logos, Names, and Photos — Doctrine

**Scope:** Proof element, page-level and section-level. Run it on the whole page (does the page spotlight well-known clients) or on a specific "trusted by" block. Optional and product-dependent: many products have no famous clients, and that is not a flaw, so treat absence as an upside opportunity to suggest, not a gap to penalize.

**Overlap note:** This overlaps Expert Endorsements and Media Placements. The line: this element is logos, names, and photos of well-known brands or people who USE your product (with optional short quotes); Expert Endorsements is a formal endorsement quote or partnership from a well-known expert who vouches for you; Media Placements is being featured by news outlets. A well-known client's photo plus quote can serve more than one of these, so credit it under each and do not double-penalize.

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- If well-known brands, experts, or celebrities use your product, spotlight them as proof. Recognizable users borrow their credibility to your product.
- It does not have to be the biggest celebrities in the world. Well-known "micro-celebrities" who are widely recognized in your specific niche work just as well.

### Formats (a menu)
- A simple "Trusted by" or "As seen on" logo or name banner.
- Photos of well-known clients (athletes, celebrities) using or wearing the product, ideally with their handles.
- A grid of the clients, shows, or brands you have worked with.
- Big brand logos combined with testimonials and specific results.
- A spotlight on specific clients, each with a short testimonial plus their achievements or follower counts.

### What makes it stronger
- Add credibility markers where a client may be unknown to the reader: their achievements, titles, or follower counts. This lets an unfamiliar name still carry weight.
- Get a short testimonial or quote from these clients where you can, not just their name.
- Include photos, especially of the client actually using or wearing the product (which also proves they use it).

### How to get them (guidance, not scored)
- If a well-known person in your industry proudly uses your product, reach out and ask if you can mention their name (and, if possible, get a quick testimonial).

## Examples

- **Logo banner (Breathwrk):** "Trusted by leading organizations and millions of users," followed by recognizable logos (WSJ, Forbes, Vogue, Bloomberg, Healthline). — why it works: a clean logo wall establishes credibility at a glance.

- **Athlete photos with handles (HiStrips):** a grid of athletes using the product with their handles and sports ("@carlitosalcarazz, Tennis Player"), plus "Loved by 150K+ athletes." — why it works: recognizable athletes shown using the product prove that credible users trust it.

- **Photos of clients wearing the product (Bellabeat):** "As Seen On…" with photos of named women wearing the wearable. — why it works: photos of real, named people wearing the product are strong, believable proof.

- **A grid of clients worked with (Podcast Marketing Academy):** "Some shows I've worked with," a large grid of recognizable podcast cover art. — why it works: the sheer grid of recognizable clients signals a strong track record.

- **Big logos plus expert testimonials (Mobility Coach):** "trusted by world-class athletes, Fortune 500 companies, and all 4 sectors of the US military," a logo row (Arsenal, US military branches, pro teams), plus testimonials from recognizable experts with follower counts (Joe Rogan, Georges St-Pierre). — why it works: it stacks heavyweight brand logos with named-expert testimonials and their reach.

- **Brand logos plus testimonials and results (Notion):** "Trusted by teams that ship," with each brand carrying a quote or a result ("Toyota: reduced timelines by 3x"). — why it works: recognizable brands, each with a specific quote or result, prove the product at scale.

- **Spotlighted clients with achievements (Pliability):** "Trusted by champions," spotlighting an athlete with her achievements (6x CrossFit Games Champion, Olympian) and a short quote, plus a sidebar of other athletes. — why it works: it adds the achievements so an unfamiliar reader instantly grasps how impressive the client is, then adds their quote.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Features logos of well-known companies or brands:** Where the product has them, does the page feature logos of recognizable companies or brands that use it? MET when a real logo block is present. PARTIAL when weak or buried. Set aside when the product genuinely has no well-known brand clients (not a flaw; suggest building toward it). Never fabricate. (visual — audit must ask the user) (product-dependent)
2. **Names specific well-known clients:** Does it name specific well-known clients (experts, celebrities, or niche micro-celebrities) who use the product? MET when specific recognizable names are used. PARTIAL when vague ("used by top athletes") with no names. Set aside when there genuinely are none (suggest as upside). (product-dependent)
3. **Adds credibility markers for lesser-known clients:** Where a named client may be unknown to the reader, does it add credibility (achievements, titles, follower counts)? MET when unfamiliar clients carry these markers. PARTIAL when only some do. MISSING when names are dropped with no context. (product-dependent — judged only where named clients exist)
4. **Includes photos of the clients:** Are there photos of these clients, ideally using or wearing the product? (visual — audit must ask the user) (product-dependent)
5. **Includes testimonials or quotes from the clients:** Are there short testimonials or quotes from these clients, not just their names? MET when quotes are present. PARTIAL when only names or logos appear. Set aside when there are no named clients. (product-dependent)
