# Language of the Audience — Doctrine

**Scope:** Editing/craft, section and page. Run it on a single section (does this section use the audience's real words) or across the whole page. It is one of the editing elements a deep section audit should apply, and it also runs at the page level.

## Principles (rules)

### The goal: resonance
- When copy resonates, the reader feels you truly understand them, exactly where they are and what they are going through. The highest praise is a client saying it felt like you were reading their mind.
- The job of the copy is to show the reader you deeply understand their problem, their desires, and the obstacles in their way, and that you have a solution.
- You get that resonance by aligning the copy with your customer research (surveys, Reddit threads, customer interviews, reviews). If your research shows 8 of 10 people struggle with the same specific thing, write about that exact thing, and 8 of 10 readers will recognize themselves.

### Editing method (not scored)
- You do not need to reference research constantly while writing the first draft. Instead, edit each section with the relevant research open side by side: the problem section against what people said, thought, and felt about the problem; the promise section against their burning desires; and so on.

### 1. Match the exact language of the audience
- Do not summarize what the audience is going through. Use their exact words wherever you can.
- Generic: "you struggle with losing weight." That earns a shrug ("I guess"). Real: their specific words ("you know how hard it is to lose the last five pounds... you start becoming insanely hungry... the things that worked before just aren't working... you keep waking up at night feeling hungry... thinking about food all the damn time"). That earns "that's exactly my situation, now you have my attention."
- The closer the copy sits to what real people actually said, thought, and felt, the more it resonates and the higher it converts.

### 2. Include direct quotes from the audience
- Beyond matching their language, paste actual quotes from your research into the copy, as bullets or as screenshots.
- A common setup is a preframe ("Do any of these ring true for you?" or "if you've ever felt like...") followed by a few bullets of their real words.
- Keep the quoted language intact. Anonymize it if needed, but do not tweak the wording beyond minor grammar fixes, and do not tidy it into your own voice.
- You can go further and paste screenshots of real research (Reddit threads, emails, comments), pulling out the most telling quote. Real, unedited screenshots are especially believable.

## Examples

- **Their exact worries as bullets (Social Accelerator):** "Do any of these ring true for you? Do you overthink social situations? Do you avoid speaking up (or self-edit when you do)? Do you run out of things to say during small talk? Do you replay awkward moments over and over after the fact?" — why it works: these are the audience's own words lifted from research, so readers recognize themselves line by line.

- **Research quotes framed as a survey (Fluent in 3 Months):** "We've asked thousands of language learners about their biggest barriers," then their verbatim quotes ("I'm scared of making mistakes and looking stupid," "I bought a million and one books but I don't have the motivation to use them"). — why it works: the exact, unpolished quotes resonate and add credibility.

- **The reader's inner thoughts, verbatim (A-Z Podcast Launch Plan):** "these prickly little thoughts keep getting in the way: 'I'm just not techy enough...', 'What if I launch and nobody listens?', 'Aren't there, like, a GAZILLION podcasts already?'" — why it works: it captures the exact self-talk of the audience, which reads as mind-reading.

- **Real Reddit screenshots as the lead (Finishers Formula):** pasted Reddit comments ("I can do anything I want to do, but I don't want to do anything") with the most telling quote pulled out. — why it works: real, unedited screenshots of the audience's own words are more believable and resonant than anything paraphrased.

- **Anonymized email screenshots as proof and voice (Master Market Sizing):** "candidates get rejected due to simple estimation questions ALL THE TIME," backed by a real (anonymized) email from a candidate describing exactly how a market-sizing question cost them an offer. — why it works: the email is in the audience's own voice and doubles as proof that the problem is real and common.

- **Screenshots for both the problem and the bad advice (Critical Bass Line Challenge):** a Reddit post ("I'm stuck in a bass rut and I need help... I've been playing the same 5 riffs over and over") plus a screenshot of the unhelpful conventional advice. — why it works: it uses the audience's real words for the problem and shows the real (weak) advice they have been given.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Uses the audience's exact language:** Does the copy use the audience's actual words for their problem, desires, and obstacles, rather than generic summaries? MET when it clearly reflects how real people describe their situation (specific and recognizable). PARTIAL when it is accurate but mostly generic. MISSING when it only summarizes in the writer's own words. (May be set aside for a short or purely functional section with no room for audience-voice phrasing.)
2. **Includes direct quotes from research:** Does the copy include direct quotes from the audience (as bullets or screenshots), especially where it describes the problem or the objections? MET when real quotes are used where they fit. PARTIAL when quotes are present but few or lightly used. MISSING when there are none where they would clearly strengthen the copy. (product-dependent — depends on the research available; never fabricate a quote.)
3. **Quoted language is kept intact:** (violation check) Are the quotes kept in the audience's own words (anonymized if needed, with only minor grammar fixes), rather than tidied into the writer's voice? MET when the quotes read as authentically theirs. PARTIAL when they feel lightly polished. MISSING when they have clearly been rewritten into the writer's voice (which kills the resonance). Set aside where no direct quotes are used.
