# How it Works — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose and placement
- After introducing the product, explain how it works. Introduce all the core features at a high level. Every product needs this section, whether it is a course, a coaching program, a supplement, software, or a physical product.

### What to cover
- For each feature, explain how it works, and speak to the benefit, not just the feature. Turn features into benefits with the "so what? why should I care about this?" question ("two coaching calls a month" becomes "get support wherever you are in the program").
- Keep it high level here. You can deep-dive each feature later (in Feature Deep Dives), or go a little deeper here if you want a more concise page.

### Format
- Number the features (1, 2, 3, 4) or lay them out clearly. For a process or cohort product, a numbered step list works well (choose your date, block your calendar, join, get certified).
- Include a visual for each feature: a screenshot, graphic, or icon. For a physical product, a graphic showing how to use it (scoop, shake, drink). For software, videos or gifs of it in action.

## Examples

- **Numbered features with benefits (Social Accelerator):** five numbered features, each explained at a high level and paired with a benefit ("2x per month, hop on a 60-minute group Zoom call and get support for wherever you are in the program"). — why it works: it introduces each feature clearly and answers "why should I care?" for every one.

- **Feature to benefit (How to Talk to Anybody):** "body-language analysis so you become 10X more interesting, without changing a word." — why it works: a clean feature-to-benefit turn using "so you," which is exactly how to keep this section from being a dry feature list.

- **Features framed as reasons/benefits (Second Brain Membership):** "six great reasons to join," each a benefit ("access to experts through real-time interaction... so you define your own way that fits your needs"). — why it works: it frames the features as benefit-led reasons the reader cares about.

- **Benefit grid plus a numbered process (Earnable):** a design-forward, benefit-driven feature grid, plus a numbered "How Earnable Works" (find your idea, create an offer, get customers, grow income, live your rich life). — why it works: the numbered process makes the path clear, and every feature leads with a benefit.

- **A numbered step list (WHY School):** step 1 choose your cohort, step 2 block your calendar, step 3 receive your contract, step 4 join the live sessions, step 5 get certified. — why it works: for a process or cohort product, a simple numbered step list is the clearest way to show how it works.

- **Physical product (AG1, CalmRooty, LARQ):** a few numbered steps with photos (mix one scoop, shake, enjoy). — why it works: physical products still need a How it Works, and a few numbered steps with photos show how simple it is.

- **Software (Holo, Macrofactor):** a feature breakdown with screenshots ("input your URL, swipe ideas, edit, download and publish"). — why it works: for software, screenshots and a short feature breakdown show it in action.

## Scoring criteria

*Numbering is stable across revisions: a missing number is a criterion deliberately removed, not an error. Judge only the criteria listed.*

2. **Introduces the core features at a high level:** Does it introduce all the core features of the product at a high level? MET when the core features are clearly covered. PARTIAL when some are thin or missing. MISSING when features are not really described.
3. **Speaks to the benefit of each feature:** Does it answer "so what?" for each feature, speaking to the benefit rather than just naming the feature? MET when features consistently carry a benefit. PARTIAL when some do and some are bare labels. MISSING when it is a dry feature list with no benefits.
4. **Structures the features clearly:** Are the features numbered or laid out in a clear, scannable structure (or a clear step list for a process product)? MET when the structure is clear. PARTIAL when it is somewhat cluttered. MISSING when it is an undifferentiated block.
5. **Includes appropriate visuals:** Does it include the right visuals (a screenshot, graphic, or icon per feature; a how-to graphic for a physical product; videos or gifs for software)? (visual — audit must ask the user)
