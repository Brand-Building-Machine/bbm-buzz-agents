# Guided Tour — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose and when to use
- The Guided Tour is a video that walks readers through how the product works. Most pages do not need one; for a cheap impulse-buy it can even be counterproductive.
- Use one when: you sell a flagship program worth thousands (where people read the whole page carefully), the product has features that are easier to show on video than in text, you sell an app or software and want to show it in action, your audience is used to watching you on video, or you want to keep the page shorter (the video can replace a lot of copy).

### Structure
- A classic structure: a quick welcome, what to expect from the video, then the bulk of the video on feature deep dives, and a quick personal outro with a soft invitation.
- For each feature deep dive, cover what the feature is, who it is for, how it works (show it visually where you can), and the benefit to the client.
- Reiterate the key problems and desires early, and weave in proof (quick testimonials or results), ideally both early and near the end.

### Craft
- The thumbnail matters: make it attention-grabbing, or people will not watch. Add a headline above the video that says what it is ("here's a sneak peek inside").
- Include captions or subtitles, and keep the video fast-paced and very visual.
- Position it right before or within the "How it Works" section (it can also sit at the very top of the page).

### Audit dependency
- A Guided Tour is a video. To audit it, ask the user for the video's transcript or a written outline, and score that against the structure below. The thumbnail, captions, and pacing cannot be read from a transcript, so confirm those with the user. If the user has neither a transcript nor an outline, treat this as a self-report checklist and ask which elements the video includes.

## Examples

- **A classic feature-driven tour (Podcast Marketing Academy):** a quick welcome, what to expect, what the membership area looks like after login, then feature deep dives (the home feed so you don't miss anything, the members page so you can find podcasters to connect with, the growth plan so you follow the exact steps, the chat channels so you get answers, the events so you get feedback), then a soft outro. — why it works: it is friendly and casual, spends most of the time on feature deep dives that pair each feature with its benefit, and ends on a soft invitation.

- **A tour that works like a whole page (Copywriting Course):** a welcome and what to expect, then the audience, the desire, the problem, some education, the offer, the benefits, how it works feature by feature with benefits, proof placed strategically (10,000+ students, 93% saw a return), the unique mechanism (the Copywriting Roadmap), more proof, and a final invitation. — why it works: it is fast-paced and visual, and it works like a whole sales page in video form, which can replace a lot of written copy for a higher-priced product.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Headline above the video:** Is there a headline above the video that explains what it is ("here's a sneak peek inside")? MET when the headline clearly frames the video. PARTIAL when it is vague. MISSING when there is none.
2. **Opens with a welcome, what to expect, and the key problems and desires:** Does the video open with a quick welcome, what to expect, and a reminder of the key problems and desires? MET when it clearly does. PARTIAL when part is missing. MISSING when it opens cold.
3. **Walks through the key features (what, who, how, benefit):** Does it walk through the key features, each with what it is, who it is for, how it works, and the benefit? This is the core of the tour. MET when the features are covered with their benefits. PARTIAL when features are shown but benefits are missing, or only some features are covered. MISSING when it does not walk through the features.
4. **Includes proof:** Does it include proof (quick testimonials or results), ideally early and near the end? MET when real proof appears. PARTIAL when thin or only in one spot. (product-dependent — set aside if there is genuinely no proof to show; never fabricate.)
5. **Ends with a soft invitation:** Does it end with a quick, soft invitation to join? MET when there is a clear, soft invitation. PARTIAL when the ending is abrupt. MISSING when there is no invitation.
6. **Attention-grabbing thumbnail:** Does the video have an attention-grabbing thumbnail? (visual — audit must ask the user)
7. **Captions or subtitles:** Does the video have captions or subtitles? (visual — audit must ask the user)
8. **Fast-paced and very visual:** Is the video fast-paced and very visual, so it holds attention? (visual — audit must ask the user)
