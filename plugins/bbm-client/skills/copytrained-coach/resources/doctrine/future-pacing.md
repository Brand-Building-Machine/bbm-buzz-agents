# Future Pacing — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- As an alternative to the Client Journey, Future Pacing walks the reader through the results they can expect over time. It gets clients excited early, and it also manages expectations and helps keep them in a program for the long haul.
- It works especially well for memberships, apps, and subscription products. This is an optional section.

### What it includes
- Clear, specific time frames (for example 1 month, 6 months, 1 year, or 7, 30, 60, 90 days).
- Specific, believable benefits or milestones at each time frame, pulled from research and in the audience's real language.
- A clear visual (a timeline or a table).

## Examples

- **1 month / 6 months / 1 year (Lean In Make Bank):** "1 month from now: you're more self-aware about the money beliefs that keep you small... 6 months from now: you've raised your fee with existing clients... 1 year from now: you've stepped into the person you truly are." — why it works: clear time frames with specific, felt milestones at each, escalating from self-awareness to a transformed identity.

- **A simple benefits table over time (Pliability):** First Week (reduced stiffness, improved sleep), 60 Days (improved range of motion, increased flexibility), 6 Months (greater training capacity, improved athletic performance). — why it works: a simple table showing believable, escalating benefits over time, which suits a subscription app.

- **Granular time frames (Mobility Coach, "30/60/90 Benefits"):** 7 Days, 30 Days, 60 Days, and 90 Days, each with a column of concrete benefits. — why it works: even more granular time frames, each with concrete benefits, which builds excitement and sets clear expectations.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Walks the reader through results over specific time frames:** Does it walk the reader through results over clear, specific time frames (1 month / 6 months / 1 year, or 7 / 30 / 60 / 90 days)? MET when the time frames are clear and specific. PARTIAL when only loosely defined. MISSING when there are no real time frames.
2. **Lists benefits or milestones at each time frame:** Does each time frame carry specific benefits or milestones? MET when each does. PARTIAL when only some do. MISSING when the time frames are empty labels.
3. **The benefits are specific and believable:** Are the benefits specific and believable, pulled from research, rather than vague? MET when they read as real, recognizable outcomes. PARTIAL when accurate but generic. MISSING when they are vague or overblown.
4. **Presented as a clear visual:** Is it presented as a clear visual (a timeline or a table)? (visual — audit must ask the user)
