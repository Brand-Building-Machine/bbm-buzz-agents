# Testimonial Flood — Doctrine

**Scope:** Proof element, page-level and section-level. Run it on the whole page (does the page include one or more testimonial floods) or on a specific flood block. Placement judgments need page context (a flood usually sits after the product introduction).

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- A Testimonial Flood is a section that stacks tens of testimonials together. Once you have lots of testimonials, gather them into a flood so the sheer volume of proof does the persuading for you.
- Strong proof does the hard work for you. Instead of writing more promises, you let a wall of happy clients make the argument. Some of the strongest pages run close to a 50-50 ratio of testimonials to copy.

### What makes a flood strong
- Variety and visual attractiveness. A mix of formats lands harder than one: written quotes, screenshots, video, and before/afters.
- Screenshots work especially well. Raw, unpolished screenshots (community posts, Slack messages, emails) are believable precisely because they look real. A flood does not need to be pretty to work.
- A strong headline on top of the flood. Frame it with a line like "The results don't lie," "Don't take my word for it," or "A wall of love from our students."

### Types of floods (you can have several)
- A written / screenshot flood (community posts, Slack, emails, social replies).
- A video testimonial flood (a row or grid of client videos).
- A before-and-after flood (side-by-side transformation images or split-screen videos).

### Placement
- Place the main flood after the product introduction, to immediately prove the product works. This is also where most of your written and video testimonials, and potentially case studies, can live.
- With multiple floods, sprinkle them through the second half of the page (after the offer): a section for before-and-afters, one for case studies, one for video testimonials, and one for written or screenshot testimonials.

## Examples

- **Three floods on one page (Double Under Pro):** a before-and-after flood ("THE RESULTS DON'T LIE," six split-screen before/after videos), a video flood ("HEAR WHAT OUR RECENT GRADUATES HAVE TO SAY," a row of client videos), and a screenshot flood of raw community posts ("TESTIMONIALS FROM OUR GRADUATES," long unedited wins). — why it works: it stacks three different formats, each with its own headline, so different readers are convinced by whichever proof lands for them.

- **Raw and unpolished still works (Fit Mom For Life):** a wall of plain community comments ("I dropped all my weight and then some within the first 4 weeks post partum," "I already feel stronger and motivated"). — why it works: it is not designed or pretty, and it still does the job, because real, specific, unpolished wins read as genuine.

- **A "wall of love" from messages (Clients By Design):** a headline ("in case I haven't convinced you yet... here's a wall of love from our students") over screenshotted emails and Slack messages ("I haven't even finished the mini course yet, and I already closed a retainer project," "I named my price... and I got a YES!"). — why it works: the framing headline sets it up, and the raw message screenshots are believable and specific.

- **A mix of video and written social proof (1of10):** a flood of social posts and video testimonials from creators, each with a handle and a short quote ("absolutely love it," "the best YouTube tool"). — why it works: mixing recognizable names, video, and written social replies makes the proof varied and credible at a glance.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Has at least one written / screenshot flood:** Is there at least one section that stacks many written or screenshot testimonials together? (visual — audit must ask the user, since it is mostly screenshots) MET when a real flood of many testimonials is present. PARTIAL when only a few scattered testimonials appear rather than a flood. MISSING when there is no flood.
2. **Has a video testimonial flood:** Is there at least one flood of video testimonials? (visual — audit must ask the user) (bonus — if met it can raise the score; being missing does NOT count against the score)
3. **Has a before-and-after flood:** Is there at least one before-and-after flood? (visual — audit must ask the user) (product-dependent — set aside for products where a visual before/after genuinely cannot exist) (bonus — if met it can raise the score; being missing does NOT count against the score)
4. **Each flood has a strong headline:** Does each flood carry a strong headline on top (for example "the results don't lie," "don't take my word for it," "a wall of love")? MET when the floods are clearly headlined. PARTIAL when only some are, or the headline is generic. MISSING when the flood has no framing headline.
5. **The flood has variety and volume:** Does the flood carry real volume (tens of testimonials) and a mix of formats (written, screenshot, video, before/after), rather than a thin, uniform handful? MET when it is voluminous and varied. PARTIAL when present but thin or one-note. MISSING when there is no real flood. (product-dependent — some newer products genuinely have few testimonials; note that collecting more is a high priority rather than penalizing as if the copy were the problem.)
