# The Reframe — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- The Reframe bridges the emotional set-up (Twisting the Knife and Painting the Dream) into the solution or the product. It explains why the reader has not gotten results, and reassigns the cause so everything "clicks." It can also bridge the Counter-Mechanism into the Unique Mechanism.

### The core move
- Built on the "it's not X, it's Y" logic: the reader does not need more of what they have been doing (X), they need the real lever they have been missing (Y). "The problem isn't effort, it's direction." "It's not the workouts, it's the other 160 hours."
- Explain why everything they have already tried has not worked, so the reframe lands as a realization rather than a claim.
- Tease the solution and point toward your mechanism.
- Boil it down to one simple, memorable sentence. A fuzzy paragraph does not "click"; a single clean line does.

### Placement
- Placement is flexible. The reframe often lives inside another section (the Counter-Mechanism, About You, Education, or even the lead). You will likely already be reframing as you write; the job here is to sharpen it into one clear line.

### Audit dependency
- **When auditing, always ask for the surrounding context first**, specifically what the reader has already tried (the problem or counter-mechanism set-up this reframe bridges from). The "explains why they have not succeeded" criterion cannot be judged without it. If the reframe is embedded in a larger section, ask them to paste that section too. Ask, wait, then score. If they do not have it, score what is shown and say so.

## Examples

- **Effort vs direction (Social Accelerator):** it first walks through everything the reader has tried ("you've done the research, the podcasts, the videos, the top 10 tips... poured hours into trying to fix this"), twists the knife on the emotional cost ("you watch other people rise... stomach-tightening regret"), then lands the reframe: "The problem isn't effort. It's direction. You've been working hard, but going in circles, when what you really need is a path." — why it works: it shows everything the reader has already tried, makes the cost felt, then reassigns the problem from effort to direction and teases the fix.

- **Reframe inside a personal story (Time Genius):** planted in the About You: "You work endlessly, thinking more hustle will fix it. But the results never match the effort," resolved later with "My secret? I start every day knowing exactly what to focus on and what to ignore." The reframe is: you do not need more hustle, you need to focus your attention on the highest-ROI tasks. — why it works: it arrives as a lived realization inside a personal story rather than a lecture.

- **One memorable line (Fit Mom For Life):** "It's not the workouts, it's the other 160 hours." — why it works: one sentence reassigns the cause of failure from the 3-4 gym hours to the other 160 hours of the week, which instantly reframes what the reader actually needs.

- **Reframe as a bridge into proof (Earnable):** "There's a way to escape The Great Squeeze. But it means you have to take advice from winners... Why would I ask a 9-to-5 employee how to escape the 9-to-5?" bridging the Counter-Mechanism into a proof-rich personal story. — why it works: it reframes why past advice failed (wrong source) and bridges straight into proof.

- **Redefining the term itself (Pillars of Productivity):** a "productivity is NOT / productivity is" table that redefines what productivity means. — why it works: it redefines the core word so the reader starts thinking about it in a new way, which the product then delivers on.

- **Tool vs system (Notion Mastery):** under "HOWEVER!!", "It's not about the tool, it's about the system you use with it." — why it works: a single-sentence reframe reassigns the problem from the tool to the system, which sets up the product perfectly.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Bridges the set-up into the solution, and flows naturally:** Does the reframe bridge the emotional set-up (the problem and the dream) toward the solution or product, sitting naturally where it lands? MET when it clearly bridges and flows. PARTIAL when it gestures at a bridge but sits awkwardly. MISSING when there is no bridge to the solution.
2. **Built on the reframe logic (the real lever, not more of the same):** Is it built on the "it's not X, it's Y" logic, reassigning the cause from "do more of what you're already doing" to the real lever they have been missing? MET when the reframe clearly reassigns the cause to a new lever. PARTIAL when it hints at a reframe without a clear pivot. MISSING when it just restates the problem.
3. **Explains why they have not gotten results despite what they tried:** Does it explain why everything they have already tried has failed, so the reframe lands as a realization? MET when it clearly connects their past attempts to why they failed. PARTIAL when it asserts the reframe without explaining the past failure. MISSING when it never addresses what they tried.
4. **Teases the solution and points toward your mechanism:** Does it point forward, teasing the solution or your mechanism? MET when it clearly points toward the fix. PARTIAL when the forward pull is faint. MISSING when it leads nowhere.
5. **Boils down to one simple, memorable "click" sentence:** (the one job) Is the reframe crisp enough to remember, a single clean line that makes everything click, rather than a fuzzy paragraph? MET when there is a clear, memorable one-line reframe. PARTIAL when the idea is there but buried in wordiness. MISSING when there is no distinct reframe line.
