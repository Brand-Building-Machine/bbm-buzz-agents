# Sample Lesson — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- A sample lesson is one of the quickest, easiest ways to increase conversions. It is optional, and strongly recommended, for any product with lessons.

### The three rules
- It must be digestible and interesting enough that people actually watch it (roughly 10 to 30 minutes).
- Use one of your best lessons, ideally something your existing clients rave about. Do not be afraid to give away your best content; it sets the tone. A lesson that blows people away makes them eager to join; an underwhelming one can decrease conversions.
- It should ideally give a-ha moments or quick wins.

### The copy
- The copy is straightforward: a headline ("Watch a sample lesson from the course"), a short invite to watch it (to see what the course is like), and text explaining what the reader will learn in the lesson.
- Add an engaging thumbnail and subtitles. You can host the video unlisted on YouTube and embed it.

### Audit dependency
- The sample lesson is a video, so its quality, thumbnail, and subtitles cannot be judged from the page copy. Proactively ask the user: is it one of your best, most valuable lessons; does it give a-ha moments or quick wins; is it roughly 10 to 30 minutes; does it have an engaging thumbnail and subtitles? Have them confirm, then score those. If they cannot confirm, note it. Judge the framing copy (the headline, the invite, and the "what you'll learn" text) directly.

## Examples

- **Clean and straightforward (Altitude Climbing):** "Watch a sample lesson from the course," an invite to see what the course is like, then text on what the lesson covers ("how to use heel hooks effectively, avoiding common mistakes"). — why it works: a simple frame with a clear explanation of what the lesson teaches.

- **Framed as a taste of the frameworks (Ship 30):** "Want to see some of our frameworks in action?", a preview of the headline framework, with a strong thumbnail. — why it works: it frames the sample as a taste of the frameworks the reader will get, and the thumbnail invites the click.

- **Links to a full lesson, with extras (French Vocabulary):** a button to the full sample lesson, an invite to download the slides and rewatch as many times as they wish. — why it works: it lets the reader engage with the lesson on their own terms, which is a nice touch.

## Scoring criteria

*Numbering is stable across revisions: a missing number is a criterion deliberately removed, not an error. Judge only the criteria listed.*

2. **Includes a short invite to watch:** Is there a short invite encouraging the reader to watch it (a sneak peek of the course)? MET when the invite is clear. PARTIAL when thin. MISSING when there is no invite.
3. **Explains what they'll learn in the lesson:** Does it explain what the reader will learn in the sample lesson? MET when clear. PARTIAL when vague. MISSING when absent.
4. **It is one of your best, most valuable lessons:** Is the sample one of your best lessons, rather than a throwaway (an underwhelming sample can decrease conversions)? MET when the user confirms it is a top lesson. (audit must ask the user)
5. **It is digestible and interesting:** Is the lesson digestible and interesting enough to watch (roughly 10 to 30 minutes)? (audit must ask the user)
6. **It gives a-ha moments or quick wins:** Does the lesson give a-ha moments or quick wins? (audit must ask the user)
7. **Has an engaging thumbnail:** Does the video have an engaging thumbnail? (visual — audit must ask the user)
8. **Has subtitles:** Does the video have subtitles? (visual — audit must ask the user)
