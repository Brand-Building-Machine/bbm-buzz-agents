# Product Reviews — Doctrine

**Scope:** Proof element, page-level and section-level. Run it on the whole page (does the page carry a reviews section) or on the reviews block itself. Its fit is product-dependent (mainly e-commerce and high-volume products).

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose and when to use
- A product reviews section shows a star rating and a body of individual customer reviews. It is a fantastic proof element, especially for e-commerce stores or any product with thousands of paying customers.
- It is often one of the first sections a potential buyer looks for, so it carries real weight for products where buyers expect it.

### What a strong reviews section includes
- A clearly visible star rating and the number of reviews (for example "4.8 stars, based on 1,277 reviews"), ideally with a breakdown by star level.
- A healthy volume of positive reviews. Aim for at least 100.
- Verified-reviewer marks and which product each review is for.
- Customer photos in the reviews, which make them much stronger.
- Customer videos where relevant, which also hand you video testimonials automatically.

### Integrity
- Only display reviews for the specific product being sold on this page. Showing reviews of other products is misleading.
- If you target multiple markets or languages, only display reviews in the language of this sales page (do not show reviews in a language the reader does not read).

## Examples

- **Rating, breakdown, and a detailed review (CrunchLabs):** "4.8 stars, based on 1,277 reviews," a star-level breakdown, verified-reviewer marks, customer photo thumbnails, and a full review ("My kids are two years into the build box progression... so much care put into the science concepts"). — why it works: the visible rating and volume establish trust at a glance, and a specific, detailed verified review adds depth.

- **Reviews with customer photos (CrunchLabs):** reviews that include photos of the finished product in the customer's own home. — why it works: a real photo of the product in use is proof the reviewer actually bought and used it, which makes the review far more believable.

- **Reviews with customer video (CrunchLabs):** a review with a video of children using the product, from a verified reviewer ("Build boxes have significantly enhanced my grandchildren's lives"). — why it works: a customer video is the strongest form of review, and it doubles as a free video testimonial.

## Scoring criteria

*Numbering is stable across revisions: a missing number is a criterion deliberately removed, not an error. Judge only the criteria listed.*

2. **Shows a clear star rating and review count:** Is there a clearly visible star rating and number of reviews (ideally with a star-level breakdown)? (visual — audit must ask the user)
3. **Has a healthy volume of positive reviews:** Are there at least around 100 positive reviews? MET when the volume is healthy. PARTIAL when there are only a handful. MISSING when there are effectively none. (product-dependent — a newer product may genuinely have few; note that collecting more is the priority.)
4. **Includes customer photos:** Do the reviews include customer photos of the product in use? (visual — audit must ask the user)
5. **Includes customer videos:** Do the reviews include customer videos? (visual — audit must ask the user) (bonus — if met it can raise the score; being missing does NOT count against the score)
6. **Shows only relevant, same-language reviews:** Are only reviews for this specific product shown, and only in the language of this sales page? MET when the reviews are all for this product and in the page's language. PARTIAL when a few are off-product or off-language. MISSING when the section shows reviews of other products or in another language (which misleads the reader). (visual — audit must ask the user, since it depends on the displayed reviews)
