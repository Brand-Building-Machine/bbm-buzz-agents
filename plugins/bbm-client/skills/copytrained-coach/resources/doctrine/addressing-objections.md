# Addressing Objections — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- As readers move through the page, objections pop up ("does this really work?", "will it work for my situation?", "how is this different?", "is it worth the money?", "what if it doesn't work?"). Your job is to address every major objection, as soon as it arises or just before, and to back it with strong proof where you can.
- You already handle many objections through other sections: proof answers "does it work / can I trust you / will it work for me," the guarantee answers "what if it doesn't work," the competitive comparison answers "how is it different," the price justification answers "is it worth it," and bonuses answer various objections. This section is the dedicated one for the major objections that remain after the Qualifying section. Minor or less frequent objections go in the FAQ.

### How to find the objections (a process, not scored)
- Use Copy-Boarding: show your copy to potential clients, other entrepreneurs, or copy coaches, and have them write down every objection as they read. Improve the copy, get more feedback, and repeat until there are no objections left. Also mine the emails and messages you get from fence-sitters.

### How to address them
- Call out each objection directly, ideally in a headline the reader recognizes ("Will this work for me even if...?", "Fluent in 3 months, really?!").
- Back each objection with strong proof wherever possible (a testimonial from a client who had that exact doubt, a proof photo, a "testimonial flood"). Proof does the majority of the work.
- Where you do not have proof yet, explain how you designed or tested the program to ensure success, and point to the specific features or bonuses that address the objection.
- Address make-or-break objections early on the page, not buried at the end (a huge objection like "is this even possible?" belongs up high).

## Examples

- **Objections answered with proof (The Home Studio Blueprint):** "small room?" answered with a before/after photo of a tiny corridor studio, then "will it work in your room? Yes, guaranteed" backed by a grid of 3,000+ student studios. — why it works: it names the exact objection and answers it with proof, which is far stronger than a claim.

- **Objections answered with testimonials (Employee to Entrepreneur):** "Even if you don't have a business idea / you're busy / you're not confident," each answered with a real client testimonial who had that doubt and still succeeded. — why it works: a testimonial from someone who shared the reader's exact objection is the most convincing possible answer.

- **Objections answered by pointing to the program (Time Genius):** "Even if you're terrified you won't follow through," answered by naming the Follow-Through Formula, coaching support, and live sessions. — why it works: when proof is not front and center, pointing to the specific parts of the program that solve the objection works well.

- **"How is this different?" answered in depth (BASB):** "yet another expensive self-help program?" answered with 7 critical differences between this and other courses. — why it works: in a crowded market where readers have been burned, spelling out exactly how you are different disarms the skeptic.

- **Honest framing, placed early (Fluent in 3 Months):** "Fluent in 3 months, really?!" answered honestly ("yes, if you do 8+ hours a day; but our students do 45 minutes a day for a 15-minute conversation in 90 days"), placed early because it is a make-or-break objection. — why it works: the honesty makes the smaller promise more believable, and handling the biggest objection early keeps the reader on the page.

## Scoring criteria

*Numbering is stable across revisions: a missing number is a criterion deliberately removed, not an error. Judge only the criteria listed.*

2. **Calls out each objection directly:** Does it call out each objection directly, ideally in a headline the reader recognizes? MET when the objections are named plainly. PARTIAL when they are only implied. MISSING when objections are answered without ever being named.
3. **Actually answers each objection:** Does it acknowledge and genuinely resolve each objection, rather than just naming it? MET when each objection is really answered. PARTIAL when some answers are thin. MISSING when it names objections without resolving them.
4. **Backs objections with strong proof:** Does it back objections with strong proof where possible (a testimonial from a client who had that doubt, a proof photo, a testimonial flood)? MET when the key objections are backed with real proof. PARTIAL when proof is thin or only some are backed. Set aside if the seller genuinely has no proof yet (then the explanation and feature-pointing in the next criterion carries the load). (product-dependent — where the proof is a screenshot or photo, confirm it with the user.)
5. **Points to specific features, bonuses, or design/testing:** Does it point to the specific features, bonuses, or design and testing choices that address each objection (and, where proof is thin, explain how the program was built or tested to ensure success)? MET when it clearly connects objections to the parts of the program that resolve them. PARTIAL when it does this for some. MISSING when objections are answered only with bare reassurance.
