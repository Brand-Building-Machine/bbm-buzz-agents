# Market Timing — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- Every so often, real shifts happen in a market that your audience is already aware of (the AI boom, crypto, a recession, GLP-1 drugs in fitness). If your audience is already thinking about a shift, address it.
- You do this to create urgency (so the reader acts now rather than putting it off) and to pre-address the objections the shift raises in their mind.

### When to use it (the gate)
- Only use this section when there is a genuine, relevant external shift your audience already feels. If you force an irrelevant shift, or invent one that is not really moving your market, it reads as filler and undermines trust. No real, relevant shift means no Market Timing section.

### How to do it well
- Acknowledge the shift clearly.
- Spotlight the cost of waiting (the negative consequences of not acting now), which creates natural urgency.
- Back the shift with proof where the claim needs it (forecasts, statistics, trends, screenshots).
- Use the shift to pre-address the objections it raises. If it shows up mainly as a rising objection (like "will AI make this obsolete?"), you can handle it in the FAQ rather than a dedicated section.
- A big market shift is also a good reason to update older sales pages, so they address the new objections that have appeared.

## Examples

- **Cost of waiting (AI Dream Team, "The Hidden Cost of Waiting"):** "AI is moving FAST. Right now, you still have the opportunity to get ahead of the curve, but that opportunity is fading. Every day you wait to master AI collaboration, you're losing precious time you could be using to [better serve your audience, create stronger offers, focus on your zone of genius]." — why it works: it names the real shift (the AI boom), spotlights the cost of waiting, and creates urgency to act now.

- **A shift backed by a forecast (BASB Foundation):** "This state of permanent information overload has become our new normal... The volume of information flooding our brains today is just a trickle compared to what's coming. Forecasts show the volume of information created worldwide will double from 2022 to 2025." — why it works: it frames the shift the audience already feels (information overload), backs it with a forecast, and the growing problem creates natural urgency to act now.

- **A rising objection handled in the FAQ (Clients Come to Me):** "Yes, AI will change SEO. But that change will not become widespread today or even a year from now... traditional SEO best practices will most likely still play a key role." — why it works: it handles the shift as a rising objection rather than a dedicated urgency section, meeting the reader's real worry where it lives (the FAQ).

## Scoring criteria

1. **Anchors to a genuine, relevant external shift:** (violation check) Is the section built on a real, external shift the audience already feels (AI, crypto, recession, GLP-1, and so on), rather than a forced or invented one? MET when there is a real, relevant shift the audience is aware of. PARTIAL when the shift is real but only loosely relevant to this audience. MISSING when there is no real shift, or the copy forces or invents one that is not really moving this market.
2. **Acknowledges the shift clearly:** Does it name and acknowledge the shift plainly? MET when it clearly acknowledges the shift. PARTIAL when it only alludes to it. MISSING when the shift is not actually addressed.
3. **Spotlights the cost of waiting and creates urgency:** Does it spotlight the cost of not acting now, creating natural urgency? MET when the cost of waiting is clear and felt. PARTIAL when hinted at. MISSING when there is no urgency.
4. **Backs the shift with proof:** Does it back the shift with proof where the claim needs it (forecasts, statistics, trends, screenshots)? MET when the claim is backed with real proof. PARTIAL when proof is thin. Set aside when the shift is common knowledge that needs no proof. (product-dependent — where the proof is a screenshot, confirm it with the user.)
5. **Uses the shift to pre-address the objections it raises:** Does it use the shift to pre-address the objections it raises in the reader's mind (in a dedicated section or the FAQ)? MET when it clearly answers the objection the shift raises. PARTIAL when it raises the shift but does not address the objection. MISSING when it ignores the objection entirely.
