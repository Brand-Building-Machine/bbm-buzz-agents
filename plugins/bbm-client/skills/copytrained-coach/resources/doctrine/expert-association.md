# Expert Association — Doctrine

**Scope:** Proof element, page-level and section-level (often inside About You). Run it on the whole page or on the relevant block. Optional and product-dependent: most authors have no photos with influential people, and that is not a flaw, so treat absence as an upside opportunity, not a gap to penalize.

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- If you have worked with influential people in your industry and have photos of you together, add them to the page. Association with recognized figures both grabs attention and builds trust by transferring some of their credibility to you.

### The integrity rule
- Only use expert association where you genuinely have a strong relationship with the person. Do not use a random selfie you took with a famous person at a conference. A forced or fake association reads as inauthentic and can backfire.

### What a strong version includes
- Real photos of you with the influential people, with context: who they are, and the occasion or nature of the relationship.
- Where the influential people also gave you a quote, pair the photo with their short endorsement (which combines expert association with an endorsement).

## Examples

- **Photos with recognized figures, in context (Selena Soo, Rich Relationships Mastery):** photos of the author with recognized figures (Lewis Howes, Ramit Sethi, Jim Kwik) tied to real occasions ("your 2 year business anniversary party," "celebrating my book launch with the people I most admire"). — why it works: the photos are with genuine peers and mentors and are anchored to real events, so the association reads as authentic.

- **Photos with leaders, each with a quote (Time Genius, Marie Forleo):** "Trusted by respected leaders," with photos of the author alongside Oprah, Abby Wambach, and Daymond John, each carrying a short quote about her. — why it works: it pairs the association photo with a real endorsement quote, doubling the proof.

- **A photo collage with heavyweight names (Mastering The Breath):** a line listing who the author has coached (Olympic athletes, medical experts, "peak performers like Tony Robbins"), with a photo collage of him alongside them. — why it works: the collage plus the specific names makes the author's standing in the field tangible.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Includes genuine photos with top experts:** Where the author has them, does the page include real photos of the author with influential, recognized people in the industry, with context on who they are and the relationship? MET when authentic association photos with context are present. PARTIAL when a photo exists but the relationship or context is unclear. Set aside when the author genuinely has no such relationships (not a flaw; suggest as upside). (visual — audit must ask the user) (product-dependent)
2. **The association is genuine, not forced:** Does the association reflect a real relationship, rather than a one-off selfie presented as a relationship? MET when it reads as genuine (real occasions, ongoing relationship, or a paired quote). PARTIAL when it feels thin or like a stretch. MISSING when it is clearly a forced or random association, which can backfire. (visual — audit must ask the user, since it depends on the images and their framing)
