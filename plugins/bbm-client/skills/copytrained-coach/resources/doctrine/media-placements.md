# Media Placements — Doctrine

**Scope:** Proof element, page-level (usually a banner near the top). Run it on the whole page (does the page spotlight media placements where the product has them). Optional and product-dependent: many products have no media placements, and that is not a flaw, so treat absence as an upside opportunity to suggest, not a gap to penalize.

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose and when to use
- If you have a mass-appeal product that has been picked up by major media outlets, spotlight it near the top of the page with a media placement banner. It adds instant credibility for new visitors who do not know you.
- This is optional. Only use it if you genuinely have real placements. If you do not, do not fake it. Instead, treat it as something to build toward.

### What a strong media banner includes
- A clear frame ("Proudly featured in," "As seen in") followed by the recognizable outlet logos or names.
- Stronger: specific quotes pulled from the actual articles, attributed to the outlet (for example, a one-line pull-quote under each outlet name). Specific quotes carry more weight than a bare logo wall.
- Placement near the top of the page, where it reassures a cold visitor early.

### How to get them (guidance, not scored)
- Build the relationships and PR skills to earn placements at major outlets, or, for an already-sizable company that just needs extra credibility for new visitors, media placements can sometimes be bought at large outlets.

## Examples

- **Logo banner (Pliability):** "Proudly featured in:" followed by a row of recognizable outlet logos (Runner's World, GQ, Glamour, Men's Health, Yahoo). — why it works: a simple, recognizable logo wall near the top signals credibility at a glance.

- **Outlets with specific pull-quotes (Carv):** each outlet paired with a punchy quote: "Bloomberg: turn any ski boot into a personal coach," "Forbes: without doubt, a game-changer for skiers," "The New York Times: the most future-forward app on the slopes." — why it works: the specific quotes say something concrete and flattering, which is far stronger than the logo alone.

- **Outlets with descriptive quotes (Jumpspeak):** "Mashable: AI-based conversations that will make you feel as if you're chatting with a real-life native speaker," and similar lines from VentureBeat and Entrepreneur. — why it works: the quotes describe the product's benefit in a credible third-party voice.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Spotlights media placements where they exist:** Does the page spotlight genuine media placements (a banner of recognizable outlets) near the top? MET when a real media banner is present. PARTIAL when placements are mentioned but weakly or buried. Set aside when the product genuinely has no media placements, in which case this is not a flaw. Treat a set-aside as an upside opportunity: suggest building toward real placements rather than penalizing. Never fabricate a placement. (visual — audit must ask the user) (product-dependent)
2. **Includes specific quotes from the articles:** Where placements exist, does the banner include specific pull-quotes from the articles, rather than only logos? MET when specific quotes are present. PARTIAL when only logos are shown. (bonus — if met it can raise the score; being missing does NOT count against the score) (visual — audit must ask the user)
3. **Placed near the top:** Where placements exist, is the banner placed near the top of the page where it reassures a cold visitor early? (visual — audit must ask the user)
