# Buy Buttons — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose and when to reveal the price
- The Buy Buttons section reveals the price and asks for the sale. This is a must-have section.
- For products at roughly $197 and up, reveal the price near the end of the page (the last 10% or so). You are not hiding it; you just do not want the reader to decide yes or no on price alone before they have your full sales argument (like a fine-dining menu that describes the food before the prices). Low-ticket and impulse products ($27 to $97) can show the price early, and you can lead with price if price is your competitive advantage in a crowded market.

### What to include with the price
- A summary of the core features, each with a benefit.
- The bonuses, each with a benefit, and their values.
- Lifetime access and free updates, where they apply.
- The guarantee, reiterated.
- Concrete specifics (hours of content, number of videos or modules, named frameworks) that raise perceived value.
- Proof near the button (a powerful testimonial, or the number of happy clients).
- A clear call to action ("Join [product]," "Get Instant Access"), the accepted payment methods, and a contact point for questions.
- This serves two readers: it summarizes everything for those who read the whole page, and it gives hook points to those who scrolled straight down to check the price.

### Placement
- Include the section once or twice: first after the guarantee, and again at the very end (after the FAQ, Close, and any extra testimonials). It is fine for the two to be identical.

### Payment plans
- Offering payment plans (splitting one payment into several monthly ones) raises conversions: it makes the product feel more affordable and helps clients with cashflow. Present the options clearly (two buttons, or stacked), and always frame the full payment as "save $X by paying in full."
- Rules of thumb: skip payment plans below about $147; use 3 payments for $147 to $497; 3 to 6 payments for $997 and up; a roughly 20% surcharge on plans is typical. Do not advertise very long plans (12-pay) on the page; offer those only privately to long-term clients who ask.

### Tiers
- If you offer tiers, show each tier's price, who it is for, and what is included (often "everything in the base tier, plus..."). Show the number of spots left where they are limited, and use an "Apply Now" call to action for high-ticket tiers that need a qualifying application or call.

### Integrity
- Never invent values, specifics, or proof.

## Examples

- **Textbook (BASB Foundation):** "Join BASB Foundation," a one-line description, features each with a benefit, the guarantee, lifetime access, the bonus, the price, and a "Join BASB Foundation" button. — why it works: it hits every base cleanly, with a benefit attached to every feature.

- **Low-ticket with a sale price (The Home Studio Blueprint):** a product mockup, benefit-driven bullets, regular ($325) versus discounted ($97) price, bonus values, and a CTA. — why it works: it fits a low-ticket sale with a regular/discounted price and benefit bullets (though "Join [product]" would beat "Add to Cart" for a course).

- **Payment options framed as savings (Own The Inbox):** two buttons, Pay in Full ("save $34") versus Payment Plan ("$77 x 3"). — why it works: it presents the options clearly and still messages the benefit of paying in full.

- **Specifics and named frameworks (Part-Time YouTuber):** 20+ hours of content, 16+ hours of bonus training, named frameworks (the HIVES Framework, the Infinite Content Engine), and a "Join Risk Free Today" CTA. — why it works: it goes deep on specifics and named frameworks, and the CTA reiterates the guarantee.

- **Tiers with scarcity and a contact email (Body Mechanics Mastery):** three tiers with clear explanations, "spots are limited," pay-in-full savings, and a "email me with questions" note. — why it works: clear tier explanations, real scarcity, and a contact point to catch fence-sitters.

- **High-ticket application CTA (Podcast Marketing Academy):** two tiers with values and an "Apply Now" call to action. — why it works: for a high-ticket program, an application CTA qualifies buyers and lets them talk to you first.

## Scoring criteria

*Numbering is stable across revisions: a missing number is a criterion deliberately removed, not an error. Judge only the criteria listed.*

1. **The headline and call to action are clear and specific:** Does the block carry a headline that names the action and a call to action that says exactly what happens next? MET when both are clear and specific. PARTIAL when one is generic ("submit", "click here"). MISSING when the reader has to work out what the button does.
2. **Reveals the price clearly:** Does it reveal the price clearly (with regular versus discounted where a sale is running)? MET when the price is clear. PARTIAL when it is buried or confusing. MISSING when the price is not shown.
3. **The summary sells the whole stack, with specifics:** Does the block summarise everything the buyer gets, the features and the bonuses together, in specific rather than vague terms? MET when the whole offer is there and the items are concrete. PARTIAL when it summarises but stays generic, or omits the bonuses. MISSING when the buyer cannot tell what they are getting from the block itself.
6. **Includes believable values:** (violation check) Does it include believable values for the features and bonuses (not over-inflated)? MET when values are present and believable. PARTIAL when a value feels high. Set aside if the section does not use values. (product-dependent)
7. **Reiterates the guarantee and lifetime access:** Does it reiterate the guarantee, and mention lifetime access or free updates where they apply? MET when both (where applicable) are present. PARTIAL when one is missing. Set aside if the product has no guarantee and no lifetime access. (product-dependent)
8. **Includes proof near the buy button:** Does it include proof near the button (a powerful testimonial, or the number of happy clients)? MET when real proof is present. PARTIAL when thin. Set aside if the seller has no proof yet; never fabricate; screenshots are visual, confirm with the user. (product-dependent)
9. **Shows payment plan options, framed as savings:** Does it show payment plan options clearly, framed as "save by paying in full"? MET when the options are clear and the save-by-paying-in-full framing is present. PARTIAL when the options are shown but the framing is missing. Set aside if the product has no payment plans. (product-dependent)
10. **Handles tiers well:** Does it handle tiers well (each named, who it is for, what is included, spots left where limited, the right CTA like "Apply Now" for high-ticket)? MET when tiers are handled clearly. PARTIAL when thin. Set aside for single-tier products. (product-dependent)
11. **Shows payment methods and a contact point:** Does it show the accepted payment methods and a contact point for questions? MET when both are present. PARTIAL when one is missing. MISSING when neither is. (Payment-method icons are visual — confirm with the user.)
