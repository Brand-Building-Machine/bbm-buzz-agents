# About You — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose and the three narratives
- The About You section builds relatability and authority: the reader should feel both "this person gets me" and "this person can actually help me."
- Choose one of three narratives that fits your real story:
  - **Expert:** I have studied this for years and helped hundreds or thousands of people just like you.
  - **Personal Transformation:** I understand your problem because I used to have it, I solved it, and now I want to help you do the same.
  - **Hybrid:** I had this problem, solved it, then helped others (my clients), and now I want to help you too.
- Use one narrative (the one that fits your background), rather than a blur of all three.

### What it should communicate
- Aim for both relatability and authority. Weight the balance to your narrative (an Expert narrative leans on authority, a Personal Transformation narrative leans on relatability), and try to include at least a touch of both. If one is clearly missing, that is worth adding.
- Credentials should be specific and numeric rather than vague: revenue, people helped, years in the game, recognizable name-drops. Specific numbers raise believability. Never invent them.

### Structure and craft
- Open with a clean "Hi, I'm ___" and a claimable identity label ("The #1 Offline Dating Coach," "Money Mindset Expert"). This positions you before the proof. Some team or brand pages skip this; it is a strong default rather than an absolute rule.
- If you tell a personal story, mirror the reader's current struggle so they feel "I was just like you," and move through the arc: the before, the turning point, the after, and the invitation ("I built this to help you do the same").
- Whatever the narrative, pivot back to the reader at the end. Even a long expert story should land on "but this isn't about me, it's about you."
- Use visual proof where you can (photos, screenshots).

### Placement
- Placement is flexible (after Twisting the Knife, before or after Painting the Dream, or just before or inside the Product Introduction). Write it first, then fix the flow later. You can also strengthen it later with Proof Elements, so it does not need to be perfect at first.

## Examples

- **Personal Transformation (Write of Passage, David Perell):** "I used to hate writing. My first boss even told me I was 'an atrocious writer.'... Writing online changed everything for me. Now I have an audience of over 430,000... And I built Write of Passage to help you do the same." — why it works: it unfolds a clean transformation (the before, the mocked-and-improved middle, the after with a specific number), and ends on the invitation.

- **Hybrid, with a craft note (Notion Mastery, Marie Poulin):** "Hi, I'm Marie Poulin... When I tried Notion in 2017, it didn't quite click... it was a complete game-changer, I built a system that worked for my ADHD brain... Since 2019, I've taught thousands of people... Let us show you what is possible." — why it works: it packs the before, the turning point, the after, an objection (ADHD), the expertise (thousands taught since 2019), and the invitation into one section. Craft note: it switches between "I" and "We" more than it needs to.

- **Expert (The Starrett System):** a stack of credentials (co-founders of The Ready State, trusted by pro teams and the US Olympic Team, advisors to Chris Hemsworth and Navy SEALs), plus a touch of relatability ("they get it, life's chaotic"). — why it works: it answers "why should I trust them" with heavy proof, and fits a brand where the experts are not personally writing the copy.

- **Long Expert with relatability (Mastering The Breath, Dan):** opens with expertise ("the Bruce Lee of Breathwork"), drops into a human background (a curious kid, near-death experiences as a Navy diver), builds the expertise story (250,000+ people in 67 countries, bestselling books, coaching Tony Robbins), then pivots: "this isn't about me, it's about you." — why it works: it earns authority with specifics and photos, stays human and relatable, and always returns to the reader.

- **Personal Transformation into Expert (Find Love Offline, Camille):** "I'm the #1 OFFline Dating Coach... But it wasn't always that way. I'm an introvert and grew up with social anxiety..." (the Jason story, the change, helping friends, starting the business), then credentials (100+ workshops, two bestsellers, 130+ countries, 150+ media outlets). — why it works: the "but it wasn't always that way" bridge opens a deep, human, visual story that earns "I was just like you," then the credentials seal the authority.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Commits to one clear, fitting narrative:** Does it commit to one narrative (Expert, Personal Transformation, or Hybrid) that fits the author's real story, rather than blurring several? MET when it clearly uses one fitting narrative. PARTIAL when the narrative is muddled. Judge it on the narrative it uses; do not require all three.
2. **Communicates both relatability and authority:** Does the section make the reader feel both "this person gets me" and "this person can help me"? MET when both are clearly present (weighted appropriately to the narrative). PARTIAL when one is present and the other is thin. When one is clearly missing, note that adding it would strengthen the section.
3. **Opens with a clean "Hi, I'm ___" and a claimable identity label:** Does it open with a clear introduction and a claimable identity label ("Money Mindset Expert," "#1 Offline Dating Coach")? MET when it opens this way. PARTIAL when the label is vague or the opener is buried or absent (soft, since some team or brand pages legitimately skip it).
4. **If it uses a personal story, it mirrors the reader's struggle:** Does the personal story mirror the reader's current struggle so they feel "I was just like you"? MET when the mirror is clear and felt. PARTIAL when the story is present but does not connect to the reader's situation. Set aside for a pure Expert narrative with no personal story.
5. **If it uses a personal story, it moves through the arc:** Does the story move through the before, the turning point, and the after? MET when the full arc is present. PARTIAL when part of the arc is missing. Set aside for a pure Expert narrative with no personal story.
6. **Credentials are specific and numeric:** Are the credentials specific and numeric (revenue, people helped, years, name-drops) rather than vague? MET when the proof is specific and numeric. PARTIAL when it is credible but vague. Set aside if the person genuinely has no numbers to cite. Never invent numbers. (product-dependent)
7. **Pivots back to the reader with an invitation:** Does it end by turning back to the reader, with an invitation to help them do the same? MET when it clearly pivots back to the reader. PARTIAL when the pivot is faint. MISSING when it stays all about the author.
8. **Uses visual proof:** Does it use visual proof (photos, screenshots) through the story? (visual — audit must ask the user) (bonus — if met it can raise the score; being missing does NOT count against the score)
