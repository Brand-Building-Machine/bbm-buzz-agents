# Strategic Placement of Testimonials — Doctrine

**Scope:** Page-level (and section-in-context). This checks how proof is placed across the whole page, so it is run at the page level. It can also be run on a single section to ask "does this section back its claim, or answer its objection, with a testimonial right here," but that judgment usually needs whole-page context (what claims and objections the page makes and where).

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- Do not bury all your testimonials at the bottom of the page. If you make big claims throughout the page and raise new objections along the way, but keep all the proof at the end, you lose readers before they reach it, especially at the moments the copy starts to sound "too good to be true."
- Instead, place relevant testimonials right where the reader needs them, so proof lands at the exact moment doubt appears.

### The three moments to place a testimonial
Add a relevant testimonial immediately after:
- **A big claim,** to back it up. A strong claim (a dollar figure, a bold promise) raises the question "is that real," so a testimonial right after it answers that instantly. Result-based testimonials here also act as a hook ("how did they do that?").
- **An objection you address,** to eliminate it. Right after a "who this is for / will this work for me" section or an objection handled, a matching testimonial proves it rather than just asserting it (and the reader can find themselves in the specific testimonial).
- **A feature (or bonus, or tier) you introduce,** to prove it works. After introducing a specific part of the product, a testimonial about that exact part proves the claim.

### Match the proof to the claim
- The testimonial must be relevant to the claim, objection, or feature it sits under. A generic testimonial under a specific claim is weaker than one that speaks to that exact point.
- Where a claim is unusually bold or "over the top" (and might turn readers away if unbacked), backing it immediately with a testimonial is what keeps it credible.

### Collect placement-ready testimonials on purpose
- Collect these in your feedback surveys so you have the right proof for the right spot. Ask clients which features they found most useful and why, which bonuses helped most and how, and (for higher tiers) their experience with the added features.
- To get objection-answering testimonials, ask clients what concerns they had before joining, and what they would say to someone with those same concerns who is thinking of joining.

## Examples

- **Testimonial after an objection handled (Beginner Bass Challenge):** a "Who is this right for" section answering "will this work for me," immediately followed by a testimonial from that exact type of buyer ("I was a total beginner, starting something new at 70... your approach was perfect for me"). — why it works: it does not just say the challenge is right for beginners, it proves it right there, and the reader recognizes themselves in the specific testimonial.

- **Testimonial after a feature introduced (Product Flywheel, "Pre-Building"):** a new program element explained, then a video testimonial for that exact element ("From 'I'm not an expert at course design' to a curriculum outline in 1 hour"). — why it works: the proof sits directly under the feature it validates, so the feature reads as real rather than claimed.

- **Testimonials after a big claim (No-Stress Negotiation):** a headline claiming "$5,000, $10,000, even $20,000+ raises," immediately followed by a stack of specific results ("20% salary increase," "doubled my salary from $43,224 to $83,125"). — why it works: the bold claim raises "is that real," and the results answer it instantly, while also hooking the reader into wondering how they did it.

- **Testimonial after an objection, deeper in the page (No-Stress Negotiation, "I can't negotiate"):** an "Invisible Script" section addressing the belief "I can't negotiate," backed right after by a testimonial from someone who negotiated a raise "while salaries were frozen." — why it works: the proof lands exactly where the specific objection is raised.

- **Testimonials backing claims throughout (Mastering The Breath):** each benefit and claim, from "reduce stress and anxiety fast" to bolder "altered states of consciousness and mystical experiences," is immediately paired with a matching five-star testimonial. — why it works: it places proof under every claim, including the over-the-top ones that would otherwise lose skeptical readers.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Testimonials sit right after big claims:** Are the page's big claims backed by a relevant testimonial placed immediately after them, rather than left unproven until the bottom of the page? MET when the major claims carry adjacent proof. PARTIAL when only some do. MISSING when big claims stand alone with no nearby proof.
2. **Testimonials sit within the objection-handling:** Where the page addresses objections (who it is for, will it work for me, specific doubts), is a matching testimonial placed right there to eliminate the objection? MET when objections carry adjacent, relevant proof. PARTIAL when only some do. MISSING when objections are handled with no supporting proof nearby.
3. **Testimonials sit next to specific features or bonuses:** Where the page introduces specific features (or bonuses, or tiers), are there feature-specific testimonials proving those parts work? MET when key features carry their own proof. PARTIAL when only some do. MISSING when features are introduced with no supporting proof. (product-dependent — set aside where the product has no feature-specific testimonials yet; note collecting them is worthwhile.)
4. **The placed proof is relevant to what it sits under:** Are the placed testimonials actually relevant to the specific claim, objection, or feature they back, rather than generic praise dropped in? MET when the proof matches the point. PARTIAL when it is loosely related. MISSING when unrelated testimonials are used as filler.
