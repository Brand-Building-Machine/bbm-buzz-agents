# Testimonials With Screenshots or Results — Doctrine

## Principles (rules)

### Purpose
- You make testimonials stronger by including specific results and numbers, and ideally visual proof of them (screenshots, photos). This is a must-have. If you do not have result-based testimonials yet, collecting them is a high strategic priority.

### How to make them strong
- Include the specific result (a dollar figure, a percentage, a count, an exam score, a before/after number).
- Provide proof: raw screenshots (texts, emails, dashboards), photos of finished client work, or mini case-studies (the process plus the result).
- Spotlight the result so it stands out: a headline, a highlight, or a bigger number.
- Raw screenshots and photos work even without design. If you do design them, keep them readable; do not sacrifice readability for design.

### Integrity
- Never invent a result or a number, and never reword a real testimonial. Pull from your real client results. The audit assesses whether the results are specific, proven, and spotlighted; it cannot verify authenticity, so it does not score that, and it does not rewrite testimonials.

## Examples

- **A specific result plus authority (Selena Soo, via Farnoosh Torabi):** "she's helped me fill a high-ticket program at 5x the price point I'd originally imagined." — why it works: a concrete result ("5x the price point") from a recognizable name.

- **A mini case-study (a creator's 4.3M-view video):** the idea inspiration, the thumbnail inspiration, and the result (4.3 million views in a month), with the actual thumbnail. — why it works: it shows the exact process and the result, so it reads as a mini case-study, not just a claim.

- **A spotlighted dollar result (80/20 Solutions):** "$50K of new inquiries... 10x'd his profit per hour without more client work." — why it works: the specific dollar figure is spotlighted in a headline so it stands out.

- **Multiple concrete metrics (WeTracked):** "300% up ad revenue, 35% lower cost, 4:1 average ROAS, 2.7X ROI." — why it works: several concrete metrics stacked together make the result undeniable.

- **A comparative exam result (Master Organic Chemistry):** "I scored a 54/70 on a final where the class average was 20/70." — why it works: a specific, comparative number that makes the result land.

- **A results grid and a raw screenshot (Freelance Accelerator):** a grid of student outcomes ("$4,800/mo in one week," "5x her old salary"), plus a text-message screenshot ("landed a $2,400 client before finishing"). — why it works: the grid floods the reader with specific outcomes, and the raw screenshot proves one.

- **Photos of finished work (Powered Up, iPhone Photo Academy):** photos of what students built or shot. — why it works: for a creative product, the client's finished work is the result.

## Scoring criteria

1. **Has testimonials with specific results:** Are there testimonials with specific results (aim for at least 5)? MET when several carry concrete results. PARTIAL when few or vague. MISSING when none (a high-priority gap to collect). Never invent results.
2. **The results are specific and concrete:** Are the results specific and concrete (real numbers or outcomes, in the client's own words), rather than vague? MET when specific. PARTIAL when partly specific. MISSING when vague.
3. **Has visual proof of the results:** Is there visual proof (screenshots, photos of finished work, mini case-studies)? (visual — audit must ask the user)
4. **The results are spotlighted:** Are the specific results spotlighted so they stand out (a headline, a highlight, a bigger number)? MET when the results are made to pop. PARTIAL when they are buried in the text. MISSING when they are not emphasized at all. (Styling is partly visual; confirm with the user.)
5. **Designed testimonials stay readable:** If any testimonials are designed, do they stay readable (design does not sacrifice readability)? (visual — audit must ask the user) (soft — only relevant where testimonials are designed)
