*Provenance: 2026-11-04, standalone Coach v2 batch 1. Guidelines that map every kind of copy onto the section doctrine this package carries. They are not doctrine of their own.*

## How to use this file

**1. Identify first.** Before anything is judged, decide the family and the type, using each entry's "Recognise it by". For a sales page or a landing page, also decide the price point, the product kind and the traffic source where the copy signals them. A piece that fits no entry takes the nearest one, and the read line says which.

**2. The parts map to doctrine you already carry.** Every section doctrine file named here (`headline.md`, `lead.md`, `buy-buttons.md` and the rest) resolves to `resources/doctrine/<file>`. `lean-editing.md`, `deep-editing.md`, `section-set.md` and `proof-elements.md` resolve to `resources/<file>`, per SKILL.md. **Load only the files the entry's "Load" line names**, plus the editing layer the mode calls for.

**3. These entries are anchors.** Use the nearest entry as a reference point and adapt it to the piece in front of you. Work out what this piece is trying to do and how pieces of its kind normally work, and judge it within that. **★ Never mark a piece down for being a different kind of email, page or ad than the examples the doctrine learned from.** Name the obvious levers missing for its own kind.

**4. Rows that do not fit this piece.** A Conversion Assets row that does not fit what this particular piece is doing is dropped, said in one plain line in the report, and the remaining Conversion Assets rows rescale to that component's points (the arithmetic in audit.md's type layer). The test: would a skilled copywriter writing this exact kind of piece, for this kind of business, use that lever? **★ Never drop a row because the piece lacks it.** A row that fits and is absent scores zero. **The sales-page entry's rows are fixed and are never dropped.**

**5. Urgency and scarcity tactics are the owner's choice.** Countdown timers, evergreen deadlines, discounts that expire, limited spots: never judged as genuine or fake, never flagged, never penalised. Urgency is judged only on how clearly it is communicated.

**6. Design is never judged.** Layout, colour, imagery as design, form fields and checkout software are out. The words, including words on images, are in.

## Family: Emails

**How to use this family.** These are anchors. The coach works out what the email in front of it is trying to do and how emails of its kind work (an e-com promo, a SaaS trial nudge, a creator's newsletter), takes the nearest type, and scores within that kind. A row that does not fit this email is dropped in one line and the rest rescale; a row that fits and is missing scores zero. No email is marked down for being a different kind of email than the examples here. Urgency tactics are never critiqued.

**Shared by every email type.**

**Stages for the Section Map:** The Open (subject line, preview line) · The Opening (the first lines) · The Body · The Ask (call to action, PS).

**Parts, and the doctrine that judges each:**

| Part | Judged by |
|---|---|
| Subject line + preview line | `headline.md` (one job: earn the open; clarity over cleverness; specific over vague), `subheadline.md` (the preview adds something new, never repeats), plus the pair rule below |
| The first lines | `lead.md`, `hook-points.md` |
| The body | the section doctrine for whatever job the email is doing: `offer-introduction.md` (the offer), `twisting-the-knife.md` (the problem), `painting-the-dream.md` (the outcome), `education.md` (teaching), `personal-transformation-story.md` or `development-story.md` (a story), `written-testimonials.md` and `testimonial-flood.md` (proof), `addressing-objections.md` and `frequently-asked-questions.md` (objections), `guarantee.md` (risk), `price-justification.md` (price) |
| The call to action | `buy-buttons.md` (what the reader does next, made obvious), `close.md` (the decision prompt) |
| Urgency, where there is a deadline | `urgency-and-scarcity.md` |
| The editing layer | `lean-editing.md` / `deep-editing.md`, as on every piece |

**Watch-outs for every email:**
- **One core idea per email.** Two ideas dilute both. A set announced up front ("the 5 things that…") is one idea.
- **The subject and preview are read as one pair, in one glance.** The preview completes the subject, specifies it, reframes or relieves it, or adds a second reason to open. A preview that repeats the subject wastes the line; a vague subject with a vague preview ("Big announcement" / "It's finally here!") gives the reader nothing to decide on, and only works when they already know what is coming.
- **No walls of text.** Short paragraphs, real white space; the reader decides from the shape before reading a word. This is about how it looks, not short sentences, which turn staccato.
- **The PS carries something that stands alone**, because skimmers read it: a reply invitation, the guarantee, two or three testimonials, an offer to answer questions. Never a throwaway line.
- **Written to one person, like a friend.**
- **Dates, deadlines and prices are exact.** In a sequence, they are identical in every email.

### Launch email

**Its one job:** move the reader one step through the launch. Before the cart opens, that step is believing the idea and opening the next email. Once it is open, it is clicking through to buy.
**Recognise it by:** part of a sequence around an opening and a closing date; references to "doors", "enrolment", "early access", a reveal or a deadline.
**One type, two phases, told apart by one question: does it link to something you can buy?** The coach states the phase in the read line.

**Pre-launch phase (before the cart opens)**

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | the one idea, built and paid off (story, puzzle, teaching, value) | 35 |
| Conversion Assets | The open (subject + preview) | 15 |
| | The bridge: the idea points at the problem the product will solve | 8 |
| | The pull to the next email (a reply prompt, an open loop, what's coming) | 10 |
| | Proof behind the teaching (a result, a student, a number) | 7 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **No pitch and no link to the sales page** before the cart is open. A product mention, if any, only at the very end.
- **The curiosity has to be spent on the thing being sold.** A great puzzle about the wrong subject gets engagement now and loses it at the reveal.
- **Ends on a reply prompt or a clean handoff into the next email**, not a cliffhanger for its own sake.
- **Teaching gives the what and the why, never the full how** that the product sells.

**Sales phase (cart open to close)**

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | the one idea this email carries (the offer, an objection, proof, the guarantee, the close) | 25 |
| Conversion Assets | The open (subject + preview) | 12 |
| | The ask: the link early, again at the call to action, an obvious next step | 10 |
| | The offer, clear: what it is and what they get, in a line or two | 8 |
| | Proof | 8 |
| | Reason to act now (the deadline, clearly stated) | 7 |
| | Objections and risk reversal (an answer, the guarantee, often in the PS) | 5 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **The open-cart email says it is open in the subject line**, and is short: everything is a summary, nothing a full section. Short beat long when tested.
- **The open-cart email borrows the offer introduction's headline, not the page's top headline**, because it is talking to someone already persuaded.
- **Link whenever the product is named**, usually more than once.
- **The last-call email is a reminder, not a sales email:** it opens on the offer and the deadline in the first two lines, lists benefits instead of re-arguing, and gives a genuine out ("it's fine if now isn't the time").
- **The PS on the open-cart email invites questions:** the replies become the objection emails.

**Load:** `headline.md`, `subheadline.md`, `lead.md`, `hook-points.md`, the one body doctrine file the email's job needs, `buy-buttons.md`, `close.md`; `urgency-and-scarcity.md` in the sales phase; the editing layer.

### Flash sale email

**Its one job:** get a warm reader to take a time-limited deal before it ends.
**Recognise it by:** a discount, a price, or a bonus with a short, dated window; usually standalone or two or three emails.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | why this, why now, in a few lines | 15 |
| Conversion Assets | The open: the deal is in the subject or the preview | 15 |
| | The deal, exact: what, the price, the saving, what's included | 15 |
| | Reason to act now: the end, clearly stated | 12 |
| | The ask | 12 |
| | Proof | 6 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **The reader learns the deal without opening.** The subject or preview names it plainly.
- **The end is exact** (date, time, time zone) and the same in every email of the sale.
- **A stated reason for the sale** (a birthday, an anniversary, a season) lifts it. Suggested where it fits; never scored as missing.
- **Short.** The reader already knows the product; re-arguing the whole case gives them more places to stop before the link.

**Load:** `headline.md`, `subheadline.md`, `lead.md`, `offer-introduction.md`, `price-justification.md`, `urgency-and-scarcity.md`, `buy-buttons.md`; the editing layer.

### Cart abandonment email

**Its one job:** bring back someone who started checking out and left, and get them to finish.
**Recognise it by:** "you left something", "still thinking about it?", a link straight back to checkout; sent automatically hours or days after the abandon.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | names the likely hesitation and answers it | 15 |
| Conversion Assets | The ask: one link back to their checkout, near the top | 15 |
| | Objections and risk reversal: the reason they stopped (price, doubt, timing) answered, the guarantee | 15 |
| | The open | 12 |
| | Proof | 10 |
| | The offer, reminded: what they were about to get | 8 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **It reads like a person, not a system.** "You left something in your cart" is what every store sends.
- **It answers why they stopped, not why the product is good.** They already read the page; the hesitation is the job.
- **In a series of two or three,** each one does a different job (help or a question, then the main objection, then a last nudge), and they don't repeat each other.
- **Short, one link, one ask.**

**Load:** `headline.md`, `subheadline.md`, `lead.md`, `addressing-objections.md`, `guarantee.md`, `written-testimonials.md`, `buy-buttons.md`; the editing layer.

### Onboarding email

**Its one job:** get a new customer or subscriber to their first step and first win, and confirm they made the right decision.
**Recognise it by:** sent right after a purchase or a sign-up; "welcome", "here's how to get started", login details, "your first step".

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | makes the first step feel doable and worth doing | 25 |
| Conversion Assets | The next action: one clear step, made easy | 15 |
| | The open | 12 |
| | The first win, or the promise reaffirmed | 10 |
| | Reassurance: people like them succeeded | 8 |
| | What happens next, and when | 5 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **One next step, not ten.** A welcome email with a list of everything they can do gets none of it done.
- **It does not re-sell what they already bought;** it confirms the choice and moves them to use it.
- **A lead-magnet welcome delivers the thing first,** in the first lines.
- **A reply prompt** ("what made you sign up?") turns the welcome into research.

**Load:** `headline.md`, `subheadline.md`, `lead.md`, `future-pacing.md`, `how-it-works.md`, `buy-buttons.md` (as the next-step call to action); the editing layer.

## Family: Ads

**How to use this family.** These are anchors. An e-com product ad, a SaaS trial ad, a local service ad and a course ad all work differently; the coach works out the ad's own kind and scores within it. Urgency tactics are never critiqued. The image itself is never judged as design; only the words on it are.

**The parts of an ad, and the doctrine that judges each:**

| Part | Judged by |
|---|---|
| The creative (the words on the image), and the headline under it | `headline.md` (one job: stop the scroll and earn the next line), `hook-points.md`, plus the creative watch-outs below |
| The first line of the primary text (what shows before "See more") | `lead.md`, `hook-points.md` |
| The body | by its job: `twisting-the-knife.md` (recognition), `the-reframe.md` and `old-way-new-way.md` (the old explanation falls short), `unique-mechanism.md` / `counter-mechanism.md` (the new explanation), `offer-introduction.md` (the fit), `written-testimonials.md` and `before-and-afters.md` (proof), `qualifying.md` (who it's for) |
| The ask | `buy-buttons.md` (what happens next, made obvious) |
| The editing layer | `lean-editing.md` / `deep-editing.md` |

**Stages for the Section Map:** The Creative (on-image copy, headline) · The Hook (the first line) · The Body · The Ask.

**Watch-outs for every ad:**
- **One argument per ad.** Two ideas is two ads; one argument may pull many kinds of support.
- **Specific beats big.** A precise claim with a mechanism behind it stops the scroll; "the best" is invisible.
- **The first ~125 characters carry the hook and the core promise**, because that is what shows on mobile before "See more".
- **★ The turn rule.** A creative that opens on the problem carries the turn (the product or the promise) inside its own copy, or the body's first line does it as its pair. A problem line with neither is unfinished.
- **The creative and the first line work as a pair:** when the creative carries the proof, the first line opens on the problem, and the other way round.
- **Congruence: the ad's promise is the page's promise.** When the landing page is pasted or attached, or the user asks the coach to read it, the coach reads it and checks that the click lands on what the ad promised, and that every number in the ad matches the page.
- **Congruence means nothing in the ad contradicts the page.** It never means the ad should copy the page's headline, hook or wording, and a win never says "lead with what your page leads with". A fix borrows a fact from the page, never its sentences: an ad is written short and fast, and page copy is written long.
- **Problem before product works for cold traffic** on courses, coaching and services. A product-led e-com ad (the product, the deal, the review) is its own kind and is judged as one.
- **Qualify the reader one way:** by number, exclusion, inclusion or values, in the first line or the last third.
- **Repeat the problem, not the solution.**
- **The negation pivot ("you're not X, you're Y") is a legitimate ad shape**, once per ad. It is not flagged as an AI tell in ads.
- **Emojis, caps and exclamation marks are the advertiser's voice**, never a fault on their own.
- **Generic lines get a specific replacement, never a label.** The coach never calls copy "AI-written". Where a line is generic (could sit in any competitor's ad, says "unlock" or "transform" where a real result belongs), the finding says what's weak in plain words and gives a stronger, specific version, built from the user's research or other copy when they've shared it.

### A complete ad (creative + primary text + headline, given together)

**Its one job:** stop the right person, carry one argument, and get the click.
**Recognise it by:** an on-image line or description, primary text, often a headline and a button label.
**Long or short primary text is told apart by length and job:** a long one argues (a mini sales letter); a short one points (one idea, then the click). The Argument is judged by that shape.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | the one argument: for a long ad, the belief chain; for a short ad, the one idea | 30 |
| Conversion Assets | The stop: the creative and the first line, as a pair | 15 |
| | The ask: what happens next, verb first | 10 |
| | Proof | 8 |
| | The turn to the product or promise | 6 |
| | The offer, clear | 6 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs (beyond the family's):
- **A long ad runs the belief chain, in order:** recognition, feeling understood, the old explanation falls short, the new explanation lands, what a real fix needs, the product fits, proof, the ask. **The old-explanation step is the most skipped**; skip it and the product lands as a hard sell.
- **A long ad for a paid product carries the offer the way the page states it**, three to six bullets of what's included.
- **A short ad is never a condensed long ad.** One idea in two to four lines, the product in one line, one promise or one proof, the ask.
- **For mid and high ticket, proof has to be there.** For low ticket, the price and the access can stand in for it.

**Load:** `headline.md`, `hook-points.md`, `lead.md`, the body doctrine for the ad's argument (usually `twisting-the-knife.md`, `the-reframe.md`, `unique-mechanism.md`, `offer-introduction.md`), `written-testimonials.md`, `qualifying.md`, `buy-buttons.md`; the editing layer.

### Primary text alone

The complete ad's rows, with **The stop** judged on the first line alone. The creative's watch-outs drop out.

### Creatives (on-image copy), one or a set

**Its one job:** stop the right person mid-scroll, and open a gap the ad will close.
**Recognise it by:** one to three lines, or a list, a versus or a step layout described as image text.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | there is an idea in the line, not a fact or a description of the product | 25 |
| Conversion Assets | The stop: would it stop this audience | 20 |
| | Specific: a number, a result, a concrete picture of their situation | 13 |
| | The turn (for a problem line) | 12 |
| | Read in one second: little text, the first line biggest | 10 |
| Craft | | 20 |

Watch-outs:
- **A fact on an image is a sales-page sentence, not a creative.** "AI is only as good as the data you give it" is a fact; "Claude isn't bad at copy. Untrained Claude is." is an idea.
- **A weak creative is almost always a weak idea, not the wrong formula.** The fix goes back to the idea.
- **Numbers on an image are a credential or a result, never a product spec.**
- **A set of creatives is read as a set too:** each gets its own band, and the set gets one note on whether it spans problem, promise and proof, or says the same thing ten ways. The score is the set's average.
- **Recognised shapes** (the call-out "If…", stop / start, "Still…?", the number, the reversal, the question with a number, the quote card, the plain promise, "How I…", the list, bullet, versus, offer and step cards) are named in plain words when a fix suggests a different one.

**Load:** `headline.md`, `hook-points.md`; the editing layer.

### Ad headline alone (the line under the image)

Judged on the creative's rows, as one line. With a creative or body given, it is a part of the complete ad.

### Video ad script

**Its one job:** hook in the first three seconds, carry one argument out loud, and get the click.
**Recognise it by:** spoken lines, scene or on-screen-text notes, a talking-head or UGC shape, 15 to 120 seconds.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | the one argument, compressed (the belief chain or the one idea) | 30 |
| Conversion Assets | The hook: the first three seconds, said and shown | 15 |
| | The ask, said out loud | 12 |
| | Proof | 10 |
| | The turn to the product | 8 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **The first three seconds decide it.** The opening line and what's on screen work as the pair the creative and first line are in a static ad.
- **Written to be spoken.** Flags what reads fine on a page and breaks when said: a sentence too long for one breath (roughly 25+ words, or clauses nested inside each other); things that only work on a page (parentheses, slashes, symbols like "&" or "→", a URL spelled out); a list too long to hold by ear (past three or four); tongue-tripping clusters.
- **On-screen text carries the hook for people watching with the sound off.** Where the script has none, that is a finding.
- **The family's watch-outs all apply**, the belief chain or the one-idea shape included.

**Load:** as the complete ad, plus `lead.md` read for the spoken open.

## Family: Funnel pieces

**How to use this family.** These are anchors. The coach doesn't carry a doctrine of its own for checkouts, bumps or upsells, so these entries apply the page doctrine it does carry (mostly the buy block, the offer introduction, the guarantee and the price case) to each piece's job. Urgency on any of these ("this offer disappears when you leave this page") is never critiqued. Design and checkout software (fields, layout, the payment form) are never judged; only the words are.

**★ Hosted checkouts and order bumps** (ThriveCart, Stripe, Shopify, SamCart, Kajabi, Gumroad and the like):
- **The read line names the platform** when the URL or the page shows it.
- **A lever the platform plainly does not allow is not scored.** Its row is dropped with one plain line and the rest rescale (rule 4): on these pieces, the platform is part of the piece's kind.
- **A fix that may depend on the platform is written as a condition with a fallback:** "if ThriveCart lets you add a testimonial block, put one under the button; if it doesn't, put one right above the buy button on your sales page."
- **The meaning line says the score covers the copy the seller can edit on that checkout.**

**Stages for the Section Map:** The Opening · The Offer · The Ask (and, on a VSL, the sales-page stages).

### Checkout page

**Its one job:** keep someone who has already decided from backing out, and make paying feel safe and simple.
**Recognise it by:** an order summary, a price, a payment form or a "complete your order" button.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | a one-line reminder of what they're getting and why | 10 |
| Conversion Assets | The offer, exact: what they get, the price, the plan, what happens right after paying | 18 |
| | Risk reversal: the guarantee, next to the button | 14 |
| | Reassurance: a testimonial or the number of happy buyers | 12 |
| | The ask: the button names what they're getting, not "Submit" | 12 |
| | Last-moment questions answered: billing, access, cancelling | 9 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **The summary sells the whole stack in a line each**, with the price shown clearly and the plan options framed as a saving where they exist.
- **The guarantee and a piece of proof sit near the button**, where the last doubt is.
- **Nothing new is argued here.** A checkout that re-sells the product from scratch gives a decided buyer reasons to reconsider.
- **The reader knows exactly what happens after they pay** (the email, the login, the first step).

**Load:** `buy-buttons.md`, `guarantee.md`, `offer-introduction.md`, `written-testimonials.md`, `frequently-asked-questions.md`; the editing layer.

### Order bump

**Its one job:** in one glance at the checkout, get the buyer to add a small, related item.
**Recognise it by:** a short box on the checkout with a checkbox, "Yes, add…", and a price.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | why it goes with what they're already buying | 20 |
| Conversion Assets | The first line of the box: it stops the eye | 18 |
| | What it is, exactly | 15 |
| | The price, and the case for it in a line | 15 |
| | The yes line: the checkbox reads as a yes to an outcome | 12 |
| Craft | | 20 |

Watch-outs:
- **It completes or speeds up the main purchase.** A bump that has nothing to do with what they're buying reads as a grab.
- **Read in five seconds.** The box is skimmed, not read.
- **The checkbox line is a yes to an outcome** ("Yes, add the templates so I can start today"), not a yes to a product name.

**Load:** `offer-introduction.md`, `price-justification.md`, `buy-buttons.md`; the editing layer.

### Upsell / one-click offer page

**Its one job:** right after a purchase, offer the next logical step to someone who has just said yes.
**Recognise it by:** shown after checkout; "wait", "your order is almost complete", a one-click yes and a "no thanks" link.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | the bridge: what the first purchase leaves open, and why this is the next step | 30 |
| Conversion Assets | Offer clarity and the price case | 12 |
| | The opening: confirms the purchase and hooks in the first lines | 10 |
| | The ask: a clear yes, and a clear way to say no | 9 |
| | Proof | 8 |
| | Risk reversal | 6 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **Confirm the purchase first.** A buyer who isn't sure their order went through doesn't read the offer.
- **The upsell completes or accelerates what they bought; it never implies the first purchase isn't enough on its own.** That makes the buyer doubt both.
- **One offer.**
- **The case is shorter than the sales page's** (the buyer is warm), but the price still needs a reason.

**Load:** `lead.md`, `offer-introduction.md`, `painting-the-dream.md`, `price-justification.md`, `guarantee.md`, `written-testimonials.md`, `buy-buttons.md`; the editing layer.

### VSL script

**Its one job:** the job of a sales page, delivered as a video: take a viewer from the problem to the purchase.
**Recognise it by:** a spoken script for a sales video, usually with slides or on-screen text; or a VSL on a page, transcribed (a VSL on a page is audited in its own block, after the page's).

**Scorecard: the sales-page rows and weights** (Argument 30; Proof 12, Objections 8, Risk reversal 8, The promise 6, Offer clarity and the price case 6, Reason to act now 5; Craft 25). Unlike a written sales page, a VSL is not bound to fixed rows.

Watch-outs:
- **The first seconds carry the hook:** the promise, or a picture of the viewer's problem. A viewer leaves faster than a reader does.
- **Written to be spoken.** The same checks as the video ad script: sentences too long for one breath, things that only work on a page (parentheses, slashes, symbols, a URL spelled out), lists too long to hold by ear, tongue-tripping clusters.
- **The slides or on-screen text carry the key lines** for people watching with the sound off.
- **The offer, the price and the ask are said out loud and shown on screen.**
- **Every section of a sales page can appear here**, mapped by function, with the same set-aside rules.

**Load:** as a sales page (`section-set.md` and the doctrine for the sections the script has); the editing layer.

## Family: Landing pages

**How to use this family.** These are anchors. Urgency is never critiqued. Form design and layout are never judged; the words around them are.

**Stages for the Section Map:** The Opening (headline, subheadline, the first screen) · The Body · The Ask.

**Watch-outs for every landing page:**
- **The first screen names what's offered, who it's for and the outcome**, in the headline and subheadline, before any scroll.
- **One page, one ask.** A second competing call to action (a nav menu of offers, "or follow me on Instagram") splits the click.
- **Specific over clever.**

### Opt-in page (lead magnet page)

**Its one job:** get the email address in exchange for something free.
**Recognise it by:** a free guide, checklist, template, course or quiz; a short form; a button like "Send it to me".

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | why this free thing is worth their email, now | 20 |
| Conversion Assets | The promise: the specific result the free thing gives | 18 |
| | What they get, exactly: the format, what's inside, how long it takes | 14 |
| | The ask: the button names the outcome ("Send me the checklist"), not "Submit" | 12 |

## Family: Sales pages

### Sales page (long form)

**★ The rows and weights are fixed**: The Argument 30; Proof 12, Objections 8, Risk reversal 8, The promise 6, Offer clarity and the price case 6, Reason to act now 5; Craft 25. Every existing rule applies as before: `section-set.md`, the set-aside rules, the section-specific handling in each mode file, cold vs warm. The only change: urgency is not judged as genuine or fake.

**The modifiers** shape section relevance and the watch-outs, never the rows. The coach states them in the read line when the copy signals them.

- **Price point.** Low ticket ($7 to ~$100): the price can show early; the low price and easy access can stand in for some proof; the page can be short. Mid ticket: the full argument. High ticket (~$1,000+): proof and case studies carry most of the weight; the ask is often an application or a call, and a withheld price is the owner's choice.
- **Product kind.** Courses and coaching: the existing doctrine fits as written. **Physical products and e-commerce:** reviews and ratings are the proof; specs are translated into benefits; shipping, returns and warranty are the risk reversal. **SaaS:** a free trial or freemium is risk reversal (the engine already credits honest equivalents); a demo or guided tour fits; integrations answer "will it work with what I have". **Memberships and subscriptions:** "cancel any time" is risk reversal; what arrives each month is the offer.
- **Traffic source.** Google: the first screen matches what they searched for. Email: warm, so a formal About You can be light (existing rule). Ads: the first screen keeps the ad's promise (the congruence law). Social: familiarity varies; judge from the copy's own signals.

### E-commerce product page

**Its one job:** turn a browser into a buyer of this product, usually in one screen and a scroll.
**Recognise it by:** a product name, price, variants (size, colour), "add to cart", reviews, shipping info.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | why this product, for this person | 15 |
| Conversion Assets | Proof: reviews, ratings, number sold, press | 15 |
| | The product's promise: the headline and first lines | 12 |
| | Offer clarity: what's in the box, variants, price, shipping | 12 |
| | Objections answered: sizing, materials, fit, delivery time, returns | 10 |
| | Risk reversal: returns, warranty | 6 |
| | The ask | 5 |
| Craft | | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **Features translated into benefits**: "100% merino" becomes why it matters to the wearer.
- **The questions a buyer asks before buying online** (sizing, fit, care, delivery, returns) are answered on the page.
- **Reviews near the add-to-cart**, where the last doubt is.

**Load:** `headline.md`, `feature-deep-dives.md`, `product-reviews.md`, `strategic-placement-of-testimonials.md`, `frequently-asked-questions.md`, `guarantee.md`, `buy-buttons.md`; the editing layer.

## Funnels and sequences, audited as one piece

**When:** the user gives several pieces and says "as one funnel" (or one sequence). Each piece also gets its own band and a short read in its own block. Each piece is judged by its own type's entry for that band.

**Its one job:** carry one person from the first touch to the purchase without a break in the promise.

| Component | Row | Points |
|---|---|---|
| The Argument | the through-line: one promise, carried from the first piece to the purchase | 25 |
| Conversion Assets | Congruence: each step delivers what the step before promised | 15 |
| | Each step does its own job: the average of the steps' bands, as points | 15 |
| | The hand-offs: each step's ask leads cleanly into the next | 10 |
| | Offer clarity at the money steps | 10 |
| Craft | one voice across every piece; no repetition across pieces | 25 |

Watch-outs:
- **The promise on the ad, or in the subject line, is the promise on the page.** A click that lands on a different promise is lost.
- **Numbers, prices, dates and names agree across every piece.**
- **In an email sequence, one idea per email and no idea repeated**, except the problem, which may come back; the solution is said once, well.
- **Each step's call to action leads into the next step**, and nothing in between leaks the reader somewhere else.

**Load:** the entries for each piece in the funnel, plus the editing layer for the cross-piece read.
