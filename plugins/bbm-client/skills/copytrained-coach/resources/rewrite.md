## 1. ★ No elicitation, ever

No plan to confirm, no batched questions, no stop for missing material. **A missing fact becomes a placeholder**, and the rewrite goes on. (Restated from `loop.md`'s iron rule: its "mark, don't fake" half is kept, and the ask is left out.)

## 2. Two paths

From `loop.md`'s rewrite path:

- **Named** ("rewrite the headline", "make this shorter"): make that change.
- **Open-ended** ("improve this", "rewrite it"): change what the audit found and keep what works.

With no audit run yet on this piece, judge it silently first, per the mode files' method, and never deliver an audit nobody asked for.

## 3. The voice read

Before drafting, read the piece's voice: who speaks (I, we, the brand, a named founder), to whom, register and formality, sentence length and rhythm, paragraph shape, signature words and phrases, punctuation habits (dashes, ellipses, caps, exclamation marks, emojis), profanity, the language and its spelling variant. Add any other writing the user has shared, and the brand notes' Voice section (SKILL.md). **Never shown, never asked about.**

**★ Free language means new ideas in the original's voice, never a new voice.** Every version, the bolder one included, writes its new hooks, scenes and angles the way the original writes: its sentence length, its word choice, its punctuation, its kind of headings. Headings and subheadings keep the original's shape (where its headings are plain statements and questions, the new ones are too) and change only where the audit found one weak. Named failures: a colon subtitle or a label the original never uses ("Deep Audit: The Full Red-Pen Treatment", "Before You Buy: The Straight Answers", "Built for Anyone Whose Words Have to Sell"); fragments for effect ("Calm, for once.") in copy that does not use them.

## 4. ★ The original's voice outranks never-say

If the original uses em dashes, caps, exclamation marks, "not X but Y", slang or profanity, the rewrite may use them the way the original does. Never-say filters every pattern **the original does not itself use**.

**The one exception:** when the original is plainly AI-written (its never-say patterns are dense and run throughout), keep its register (person, formality, rhythm) and drop the patterns; never-say applies as normal. That judgment is internal and never said to the user (`coach-voice.md`).

## 5. ★ Facts are never invented; language is free

Never invent a number, a result, a testimonial or a customer's words, a story that happened, a product feature, a credential, a guarantee term, a price or a deadline. Never claim what the product does not do. **A missing fact becomes a placeholder** that says what belongs there, in square brackets: `[a real result from a student]`.

Headlines, hooks, phrasings, metaphors, names for a method and scenes that picture the reader's own situation may be invented freely.

**A placeholder is for a fact only the user can supply about their own world:** a real result, a name, a price, their own story. A scene about the reader is language. It is written in plain general words ("your launch came in under what you planned"), never with invented numbers and never with brackets. Named failure: "You planned for [the launch number you planned]. It comes in at [the number it actually came in at]."

**★ Never merge two true facts into one untrue one.** Named failure: "the same 466 rules I use with my clients", when the rules were written for the Coach and the clients were coached before it existed. One line, one fact, each as its source states it.

**A fact the coach can find is used.** A fact in `context/` or elsewhere in the piece fills the line, and so does one the audit's findings carry from a page the piece links to. A placeholder is only for a fact found nowhere. **★ A linked page gives facts, never sentences.** Its wording was written at another length for another job, so nothing is copied from it into the piece, and the subagents are never given its text. **Before rewriting an ad or an email that links to a page**, the coach reads that page's text (no images, no video), unless it already did in this conversation. It gives the subagents the page's facts as a short list (names, numbers, results, price, what the product needs, guarantee terms), never the page's sentences.

**★ Regulated claims.** For supplements, health, medical and financial products, fixes and rewrites draw their claims from the page first. A stronger or new claim is allowed, and each one carries a one-line caveat beside it: "Check this claim against the rules for [supplements / health claims / financial promotions] in your market before publishing." This holds for inline fixes in audits, rewrites and brainstorms.

**★ The person and the product are facts too.** How the writer describes themselves (a title, a role) and what the product, the brand and the method are called come only from the piece or the user's material. Nothing that already has a name gets a second one; a name is invented only for a method the piece leaves unnamed, and it goes in the note on new ideas. A section that needs facts the coach does not have (a case study, a before and after, a beta-test story) may be built with placeholders for those facts; the facts themselves are never invented. This holds for fixes suggested in audits too.

## 6. Draft

Read the part's doctrine file in full before drafting it (from `gates.md`'s first condition: the file that judges the part is open before the part is written). Use the piece's `copy-types.md` entry for what the part has to do.

## 7. ★ The silent self-audit

From `loop.md`'s self-audit order, substance before surface:

1. Does it do the part's job, against its doctrine?
2. The editing layer (`lean-editing.md`).
3. The voice read, and the brand notes' standing feedback.
4. Never-say, as bounded by section 4.
5. Every fact traces to the piece or to material the user shared.

**One re-run at most** (`loop.md`'s cap). A revised draft runs it again. **Nothing is printed: no receipt, no score, no band, no report.** If something still fails after the one re-run, one honest line under the rewrite.

## 8. Show

The rewrite first. Then **one short note naming the new ideas added** that the original did not contain (a new angle, metaphor, named method, illustrative scene), so the user can keep or drop them:

*"New in this version: the 'guitar in the corner' scene and the name 'the 15-minute loop'. Both are mine, built from your problem section; drop them if you'd rather stay closer to your wording."*

New wording for an idea already in the original is never listed. Never a flag beside each line. Then any placeholders, collected into a short list (from `loop.md`'s end-list rule). Revision rounds show only what changed.

## 9. Whole-page or whole-piece rewrites

**Every whole-piece rewrite runs items 1 to 5**, part by part with one subagent per part, whatever the length. On a piece under about 1,500 words, or with 6 parts or fewer, item 1's heads-up line is skipped and item 2's "keep what works" does not apply: each part is written fresh from its findings and its doctrine. Every whole-piece rewrite runs the flow audit (item 3) and is saved to `audits/` (item 4).

1. **One line up front, then go:** it works part by part, takes a while, and uses a good share of their usage. Never a question.
2. **One subagent per part**, run with the subagent tool, each briefed with: the brand notes, the whole original piece, that part's audit findings, the path of that part's doctrine file to read in full, and sections 3 to 8 of this file. Each returns its part, its new ideas and its placeholders. **★ Keep what works:** a part the audits rated strong, or one with no finding, is briefed to stay close to the original and take only the fixes the audits named. A part moves only where an audit named the move.
3. **★ The flow audit on the stitched page**, by the coach itself, silently, after the parts are joined and before anything is shown. Read the whole page top to bottom and check: each part's opening follows from the end of the part before it; every reference back ("those same rules", "that's why", "as I said") points to something that appears earlier on the page; no point, proof, quote or line appears twice (the buy block repeats by design); the order is the original's, or the order the audits named; one voice top to bottom, the original's (every new heading set beside the original's headings and matched to their shape and register); the headline's promise carried to the close. Fix what fails, then run the checks once more. Nothing is printed.
4. **Saved** to `audits/[brand]-[piece]-rewrite-YYYY-MM-DD.md` (the configured audits directory from SKILL.md), with the new-ideas note and the placeholder list at the end of the file. **Linked for the user** with the saved file path using the runtime’s supported file links. In chat: one line on where it went, and the new ideas and placeholders in two or three lines.
5. **Offered once:** export to Word (`.docx`), to a Google Doc (with a Google Drive tool when one is connected; otherwise a `.docx` they can upload), or another format they name.

Line and section rewrites stay in chat.

## 10. Style feedback

Style feedback the user gives on a rewrite ("never use 'transform'", "shorter sentences", "we say members") is applied at once and written to the brand notes' Standing feedback, silently (SKILL.md).

## 11. The coaching line is gone

No caveat about what the coach was trained on.

## 12. No score beside a rewrite

The scoring machinery stays in audits (SKILL.md's ★ scoring rule).

## 13. ★ Another version, offered after every rewrite

**Every rewrite ends with one line offering another version, each option said in plain words.** Line, section or whole piece. The line is part of the rewrite itself, separate from the close. Something like: *"Want another version? I can make it bolder (new hooks and angles, with the changes worth A/B testing built in), keep it closer to your original (only the fixes, everything else as you wrote it), or make it shorter."* Or a direction the user names.

**The default:** a short piece is written fresh, part by part, from the audit's findings; a long piece keeps what works and takes the audit's fixes (section 9). Both in the original's voice (section 3).

- **Closer to the original** keeps the piece's own lines wherever they work and changes only what the audit named.
- **Bolder** pushes the hooks, the angles and the order, and builds in the changes the audit marked as worth A/B testing. **It stays in the original's voice (section 3), and its new material is built from what the piece and the user's material already hold:** a new scene, story or section only where the audit found a gap.
- **Every version keeps every fact** (section 5) and is written the same way as the first (section 9 for a whole piece).
- **Saved beside the first**, as `audits/[brand]-[piece]-rewrite-[closer, bold or the direction]-YYYY-MM-DD.md`, with its own note on new ideas and placeholder list.
