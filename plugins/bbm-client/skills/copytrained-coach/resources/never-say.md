# Never-Say — the copy standard

<!-- Shipped, read-only, cross-domain machinery. Governs COPY and any generated written output, not how the coach talks in an audit. -->

**What this is.** The copy standard that ships with every install. It governs COPY and any generated written output (sales pages, emails, docs, content). It does not govern casual conversation, and it is not how the coach speaks in an audit or in feedback.

**Scope.** This file governs copy produced FOR the user (sales pages, emails, content, deliverables). Audit and feedback prose follows `.brain/shared/coach-voice.md` instead: an audit write-up is generated output, but it's the coach speaking, so coach-voice governs it.

It is the FIRST layer of AI-slop prevention: it works on day one, before any scan has learned anything about the user. A per-user layer gets added on top later (see **Personal fingerprints** and **Taste calls** below), but this universal layer stands on its own from the very first draft.

---

## The hard rule: em-dashes and dash rhythm

Zero em-dashes ("—") in any copy or generated output, AND do not lean on dash-interruptions as a rhythm device. This is one of the few genuinely hard rules, not a preference: em-dash overuse is one of the loudest AI tells, and killing AI tells is the entire reason this product exists.

The tell is the RHYTHM, not just the glyph. Banning "—" while still breaking every other sentence with a parenthetical dash still reads as AI, just with a different character. Do not simply swap "—" for "-". Mirror real human punctuation: periods, commas, colons, ellipses, and parentheticals, with the occasional spaced hyphen " - " for a genuine aside, not a constant stream of dashes.

(This ban is about produced copy and written deliverables. Casual conversation is out of scope for this file.)

## Tier A — universal AI tells (shipped as standard)

These apply to anyone. There is no voice to protect: they are bad in almost any copy, so they are simply in. Each rule keeps its examples, which are what make it usable.

### Rhythm & structure
- **No dramatic fragmentation / staccato-for-effect.** "[Noun]. That's it. That's the [thing]." / "Short. Punchy. Exhausting." Write complete sentences. Use a hard short fragment only very sparingly, for a genuine beat.
- **Vary sentence length.** Never three or more similar-length sentences in a row.
- **Vary paragraph length.** Some one sentence, some five or six. Never uniform blocks.
- **Start with content, not setup.** The first sentence carries real information. No throat-clearing.
- **Use contractions.** "Don't," not "do not." Write how people talk.

### Binary & formula shapes
- **★ NO NEGATION-PIVOTS, IN ANY SYNTACTIC DISGUISE.** The ban covers the whole family, not one phrasing of it:
  - **The single sentence:** *"it's not X, it's Y"* · *"the answer isn't X, it's Y"* · *"not because X, but because Y."*
  - **The split form**, which is the same move across a period: *"It doesn't start at the top. It writes your offer first."*
  - **Every variant that negates a claim NOBODY MADE in order to set up a reveal:** *"X isn't the point. Y is."* · *"This isn't about X. It's about Y."* · *"You don't need X. You need Y."*

  **★ Absolute for generated draft prose: no negation-pivots at all. State what it IS.** Negation is founder-added in editing, and it is never generated.

  **Where a section must answer a belief the reader ACTUALLY holds**, name the belief plainly, then assert what is true. **The positive statement carries the sentence.** The test is whether a real reader arrived believing X; if nobody did, the negation is manufactured contrast and the sentence starts at Y.

  **★ THE MECHANICAL SELF-CHECK, run before any draft prose renders.** Scan the draft for negations. **Any negation that names a claim no one made is deleted, and the assertion is kept.** Not softened, not rephrased: deleted.

  **Why a rule this old needs a check bolted to it:** one evening's generated output carried **23 negation-pivots across 9 sections** with the ban already in the file and already read. **This is the model's default emphasis engine**, so it does not yield to a rule alone.
- **None of the seven expression formulas:** Cinematic Setup ("In a world where [crisis], [virtue] becomes [currency]"); Binary Split ("Most people [lazy]. The few who win [disciplined]"); Simple Switch ("Stop [old]. Start [new]"); Triple Fake Depth ("It's not X. It's not Y. It's Z"); FOMO Threat ("If you're not [X], you're already [behind]"); Invisible Work ("The real [work] isn't [visible]. It's [secret]"); Minimalist Smack ("You don't need more [resources]. You need [intangible]").

### Openers, closers, fluff
- **Slop openers:** "In today's fast-paced world / digital world / digital landscape," "As technology continues to evolve," "In the ever-evolving landscape of," "In a world where," "imagine a world where."
- **Slop closers:** "In conclusion," "In summary," "In essence," "The future remains bright," "Only time will tell," "One thing is certain."
- **Promotional fluff:** "Unlock your full potential," "Revolutionize the way," "Take your X to the next level," "Harness the power of," "Embark on a journey," "Game-changer," "Supercharge," "master the art of," "take your results to the next level," "Unlock the Secrets," "proven, step-by-step system," "more important than ever."
- **Universal over-claims:** "works for absolutely everyone," "zero risk," "thousands of successful entrepreneurs just like you." A skeptical reader distrusts them.
- **Generic emphasis-triads standing in for specifics:** "real decision-makers, real power players, real opportunities."
- **Greeting-card platitudes:** "every lesson is a new opportunity to learn and grow."
- **Fake / artificial urgency:** "last chance" on an evergreen offer.

### Filler transitions & phrases
- **Filler transitions:** "Furthermore," "Moreover," "Additionally," "Indeed," "Subsequently," "Accordingly," "Consequently," "Thus," "It's worth noting that," "In conclusion."
- **Filler phrases:** "It is important to note that," "In order to" (say "to"), "At the end of the day," "When it comes to," "At its core," "The reality is," "Due to the fact that" (say "because").
- **Chatbot artifacts (zero tolerance):** "I hope this helps," "Of course," "Certainly," "Absolutely," "Great question," "Would you like me to," "Let me know if," "Feel free to reach out."

### AI-marketing vocabulary (the metaphorical / marketing use, not the literal word)
- **Verbs:** delve, embark, endeavour, leverage, harness, navigate, unlock, unleash, foster, underscore, showcase, illuminate, elucidate, tackle, streamline, optimize, empower, reimagine, revolutionize, spearhead, capitalize, forge. (The marketing-metaphor use, "unlock your potential," "tackle your challenges," is the tell; the plain literal verb is fine.)
- **Nouns (metaphorical):** landscape, tapestry, realm, testament, journey, intersection, synergy, paradigm, ecosystem (non-technical), toolkit.
- **Adjectives (marketing buzzwords):** groundbreaking, transformative, cutting-edge, seamless, best-in-class, next-generation, unprecedented, paramount, multifaceted, elevated.

### Structural / model tells
- **Copula avoidance:** "serves as," "stands as" instead of "is." Use "is."
- **Epistemic hedging that does no work:** a verbal tic that points at what you're about to say instead of saying it ("I should note that," "it's worth mentioning"). The test is whether the hedge is doing work. A hedge that carries genuine uncertainty STAYS ("I do think," "maybe," "in most cases," "from my experience") — honest uncertainty often reads stronger than false confidence. A hedge that is only throat-clearing before the claim GOES. Just say the thing.
- **Over-qualification:** every claim wrapped in caveats.
- **Balanced framing used to fence-sit:** presenting both sides to avoid taking a position you actually hold. The test is whether the balance is real or avoidance. A genuine "it depends," where the answer really is conditional, STAYS — it's honest and builds trust. A both-sides hedge that dodges a stance you do hold GOES. Take the stance.
- **Meta-commentary:** commenting on your own response.
- **Nested clauses stacked in one sentence.** Break them up.
- **Verbosity without substance.**
- **Explanation over opinion:** describing what something is rather than arguing why it matters.
- **Questions answered immediately:** "What does this mean? It means X." Delete the question.
- **Heavy signposting:** "Now that we've explored X, let's move on to Y."
- **Compulsive summaries / mini-conclusions after every section.**

### Specificity
- **Be specific.** Replace vague adjectives with concrete details, numbers, names, timeframes.
- **The horoscope test:** could this paragraph appear in any doc about any topic? If yes, rewrite with specifics only this context would produce.

## Personal fingerprints (built per user by VoiceScan) — the shipped file holds only the slot

This layer is deliberately EMPTY in the shipped standard. It is built per user by VoiceScan, from the user's own writing and their editing passes, and stored in their personal never-say layer (VoiceScan's output, the `voice-never-say` role — resolved via `.config/index.md`; formerly §9 of the voice profile). It holds the idiosyncratic kills that make copy sound like ONE particular person and no one else:

- a word the user never uses (and its plain replacement),
- a construction they always cut,
- a habit they have trained themselves out of.

These are not universal. Shipping one person's fingerprints as blanket rules would scrub a different user's legitimate voice. So the shipped file holds the slot; VoiceScan fills it.

## Taste calls (VoiceScan asks the user to decide)

These are genuine judgment calls, not settled rules. A blanket ban here could scrub a legitimate voice, and a reasonable user might decide either way. So the shipped standard does NOT decide them. VoiceScan raises each with the user and records the decision in their personal voice layer (VoiceScan's output, e.g. the `voice-never-say` role). Until a user decides, treat these as open and lean toward the lighter touch. The trade-off note on each is what makes an informed decision possible.

- **Ban these adverbs?** — truly, deeply, fundamentally, simply, inherently, inevitably, crucially, importantly, interestingly, significantly, notably.
  - Trade-off: these are classic AI intensifiers, but several are normal English, and a blanket ban can stiffen the voice.
- **Ban the "sometimes-fine" buzzword adjectives?** — robust, comprehensive, compelling, invaluable, remarkable, nuanced, pivotal, scalable, dynamic, agile, tailored, vibrant, intricate, innovative.
  - Trade-off: banning them kills a real AI-ism cluster, but each has legitimate uses.
- **How hard to police the rule of three?** — the rule of three is a legitimate copywriting principle, so do NOT avoid it. The tell is the FORCED triad: trimming or padding two, four, or five genuinely good ideas into exactly three to hit the cadence. Let the real count stand.
  - Trade-off: the safe default (don't manufacture a triad) is close to universal; the taste call is how aggressively to flag a suspected forced three.

## What human writing sounds like

The positive counterpart to the kill-list, useful on its own. Copy that reads as human tends to use:

- **Discourse markers:** Well, I mean, Look, So, Right, The thing is, I think, sort of, kind of, Fair enough, That said, Personally, Actually.
- **Casual connectors:** And, But, So, Plus, Also, Still, Though, Anyway, Besides, Meanwhile.
- **Characteristics:** register shifts within a piece, contractions everywhere, real uncertainty ("I don't know," "hard to say"), first-person opinion, specific references (real events / people / numbers), emotional texture, wildly varying paragraph length.
