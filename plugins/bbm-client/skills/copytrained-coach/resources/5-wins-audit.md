Purpose: a short, high-impact sales-page audit built around one idea — **the score, and the five biggest wins.** No 20-page report, no line-by-line, no praise tour. The reader feeds a page, sees exactly where it stands, gets the five changes most worth making, makes them, and runs it again. The loop is the product: audit → fix 5 → re-audit → watch the score climb → 5 new wins → repeat until the copy is where they want it.

Invoke: "run the 5-wins audit" / "what are the biggest wins on this page" / "give me the 5 biggest wins" / "quick audit this page", on a sales page or any other copy, then the user pastes the copy or gives the link.

This is the fast, prioritized cousin of the deep and light audits: where the deep audit marks up every line and the light audit bands every section, this one ignores everything except the handful of changes that would move the needle most. It borrows the others' machinery (section mapping, set-aside rules, scoring bands) by reading it; it never rewrites them.

## Any copy, not only sales pages

- **Where this file says "page", read "the piece being audited".**
- **Identify the piece first**, per SKILL.md's run and `copy-types.md`'s "How to use this file", before anything is judged.
- **Scope.** A sales page uses `section-set.md`, as below. Any other type uses its `copy-types.md` entry's parts, mapped to the doctrine files the entry names, and its stages replace The Opening / The Body / The Offer / The Close wherever this file groups sections.
- **Every handling note in this file that names a sales-page section applies wherever a part maps to that section's doctrine.** On other types, the entry's watch-outs apply alongside it.
- **The score** follows `audit.md`'s type layer.
- **★ Depth follows length.** The report is as long as the piece needs. A whole-page cross-check that does not fit the piece (flow, transitions or pacing on a three-line ad) is not run, silently.
- **★ Judge the piece by its own kind**, never by how far it is from the examples the doctrine learned from (`copy-types.md`, rule 3).
- **Urgency is never judged as genuine** (`audit.md`, S2).

## The output (two sections, one page, two at most)

### Section 1 — the scorecard

**Headline:** `[Piece name] · Copy Score: [X]/100` with a color marker: 🟢 70 and up, 🟡 40 to 69, 🔴 below 40.

Under it, the scorecard: **the real decomposition of the number**, so the reader sees exactly where the page is strong and where it leaks. **Every row is a lever behind the actual score**, and the rows sum to it.

Each row shows its points out of its own maximum, with a color marker from its score over its own maximum, on the same scale as the total: **🟢 70% and up · 🟡 40 to 69% · 🔴 below 40%.** Work each row out before sending (6 out of 8 is 75%, so 🟢). Each carries a plain few-word note saying what is dragging it down, never what is nice. **A row's note stays inside that row's subject:** the Proof row's note is about proof, the Craft row's note is about the writing.

**For a sales page, the eight rows below. For any other type, The Argument, that type's own Conversion Assets rows from `copy-types.md` (after any drop-and-rescale), and Craft, each with its points.**

| Row | Max | What it covers |
|---|---:|---|
| **The Argument** | **/30** | The band-average across headline, lead, problem, reframe, mechanism, dream, education and story: does the case get built |
| **Proof** | **/12** | Testimonials, results, studies, the founder's own story, and whether the proof sits where the doubt is |
| **Objections** | **/8** | The objections a buyer actually holds, named and answered |
| **Risk reversal** | **/8** | A guarantee, or an honest equivalent, sold rather than just stated |
| **The promise** | **/6** | Is the promise big enough, clear enough, and believable |
| **Offer clarity and the price case** | **/6** | What they get, what it costs, and why that is worth it |
| **Reason to act now** | **/5** | Urgency, clearly communicated, and the close that asks for the decision |
| **Craft** | **/25** | Clarity, voice, their language, vividness, tightness, emotion, flow, hooks, subheadings |

One line of meaning under the table maximum ("strong argument, leaky close" — that register). No overview paragraph, no product summary — they wrote the page.

### Section 2 — The 5 Biggest Wins

The headline: **The 5 Biggest Wins.** Then the five, each in this shape:

- **A bolded, specific insight line** — the win itself, stated so the reader gets it before the explanation.
- 2–4 lines of why it matters and exactly what to do, with the **verbatim line(s) from the page** quoted where relevant and a **concrete example fix** (rewrite, move, addition). Illustrative examples stay clearly illustrative ("something like…, if that's true for you") — never invented specifics passed off as their facts.
- Where a win is a real gamble (a wholesale headline change, a new core promise), say so in one line: "worth A/B testing against the current version, because…". Every other win is simply stated; no win carries a risk-free label.

Prose and bullets in balance: the insight line and fix can be prose; lists of specifics go in bullets. Never a wall of text. Never fluff, pre-framing, or throat-clearing. The whole section should read like a sharp coach talking, not a report.

**Each win's body follows `coach-voice.md`'s paragraph rule:** the why in one short paragraph of about 50 words at most, then the fix as one or more bullets, with the quoted line and the example rewrite each on its own line. Never one block.

## What qualifies as a win (the quality bar — this is where the skill lives or dies)

The single failure mode: **2 of the 5 feel "meh."** All five must land. The bar for each:

1. **High-impact.** Fixing it could plausibly move conversions — not polish. A tightened sentence is not a win; a promise aimed at the wrong outcome is.
2. **Non-obvious.** The test: would a generic AI with no doctrine say this? Would the page owner already know it? If yes, it doesn't earn a slot unless your version is meaningfully sharper and more specific than the obvious take. The doctrine is what finds the non-obvious — use it (silently).
3. **Not a hallucinated gap.** Before flagging something "missing," run the **strategic-decision check**: on a page that's clearly working (strong proof, evident sales history, professional polish), a missing element may be a deliberate choice. "Add a guarantee" to a $10M page with mountains of proof is bad advice — there's probably a reason. In that situation either drop the point, or raise it as an honest question ("no guarantee anywhere — if that's deliberate, fair; if not, here's what one could do"), never as a confident mistake-catch.
4. **Specific to THIS page.** Quotes its lines, names its sections, uses its language. If the win could be pasted under a different page, it's too generic.
5. **Distinct.** Five different levers. No two wins that are the same idea wearing different words. And no two wins rest on the same material: the same testimonials, the same quote or the same passage carry one win, and a second lever on them joins that win.
6. **Actionable today.** The reader finishes the bullet knowing exactly what to change and where.

**No padding, ever.** If the page is genuinely strong and you cannot find five wins that clear this bar, deliver the ones that do and say plainly the page has run out of big wins — that's a milestone, not a failure. (In the loop, this is the finish line.) Padding with a "meh" win to hit five is the one thing this audit must never do.

**On a short piece, fewer than five is the honest count.** An ad or an email rarely holds five wins that clear this bar; deliver the ones that do.

Where the wins come from — mine ALL of these before settling on five:
- **Section-level gaps and hollow sections** (banded internally, below).
- **Persuasion:** is the promise the biggest outcome the offer can honestly own, or something smaller? Are the real objections handled? Does the CTA earn the click? Is the tone building trust or tipping into hype? Don't take the page's messaging at face value — push back where it misses.
- **Proof:** unbacked hard-to-believe claims, weak proof leading while a killer testimonial sits buried, generic proof where a specific number exists. Name the exact proof element to add or move — never "add credibility."
- **Structure:** a flow order that buries the strongest material, a mechanism that's never named, a premium tier sold only as a price-block bullet.
- **Craft at scale:** one repeated tic that infects the whole page (hype language, third-person self-reference, all-caps shouting) can be one win — named, counted, with the fix shown once.

## Scoring (consistent, so the rerun loop means something)

**The Copy Score is the component score in the engine.** Read `resources/audit.md`'s Scoring section and follow it: The Argument out of 30, Conversion Assets out of 45, Craft out of 25. **Band every in-scope section internally, under the five engine rules, and never show those bands in the output.** They feed The Argument row.

**The scorecard rows ARE the score.** They are not a visual decomposition of a separately computed number: they sum to it. **A fixed win moves its own row and moves the total by the same amount**, which is what makes the rerun loop mean something.

**Nothing in the asset rows can be set aside.** A page with no guarantee scores zero on risk reversal, and that zero is usually one of the five wins.

**When torn, round down.** The same copy always lands the same way.

Map sections by FUNCTION, not labels. **The section set lives in `resources/section-set.md`**, shared by all three audits; doctrine files are in `resources/doctrine/`. The load-bearing set-aside rules carry over; apply them silently:
- Optional-when-absent sections (client journey, guided tour, sample lesson, price justification, tiers a product could have) are set aside, never scored as gaps. Only near-universal-and-missing sections (offer intro, buy buttons, FAQ, close, guarantee-when-none-exists) count as gaps.
- A section that's scaffolded in the copy but arrives empty is almost certainly an image/embed you weren't given — not missing. Don't band it 🔴, don't make it a win ("your bonuses are missing!" when they're a graphic is exactly the embarrassing miss this audit can't afford).
- The repeated Buy Buttons / pricing block is correct by design (per `buy-buttons.md`) — never a repetition finding.
- Quote headlines usually mean a story lead — a missing eyebrow/subheadline there is usually right, not a gap.
- Don't suggest adding urgency unless the page is genuinely time- or slot-gated (then judge how clearly it reads).
- Judge cold vs. warm traffic and let it inform the calls; where a win depends on it and you can't tell, say so in one line.

## Delivery

The report is delivered in chat, in full. No file is written by default and no folder is resolved; SKILL.md's delivery rule governs. If the user asks for a saved copy, save the report as delivered, `5-wins-audit-YYYY-MM-DD.md` (or `.docx` for Word), name it yourself, and say where it went. The rerun loop still works from chat: the scorecard rows are what the user compares.

## Guardrails (carried over — non-negotiable)

- **Ask-don't-invent.** Never fabricate a number, story, testimonial, or result. Example rewrites needing facts you can't know stay clearly illustrative.
- **Improvements only.** No "what's working" section, no praise notes. Strength gets one honest clause only when a win needs it for context ("your proof is strong — it's just all below the fold").
- **The original's voice.** Every example fix is written the way this piece speaks (`rewrite.md`, sections 3 and 4): its person (I, we, the brand), its register and rhythm. Testimonials stay in the customer's voice.
- **Plain language, impossible to misunderstand.** Common copywriting terms only (headline, hook, lead, proof, objection). No internal doctrine terms, no example names from the doctrine, no jargon.
- **Coach voice** (`resources/coach-voice.md`): honest, warm, specific, never harsh, never a roadblock. Five wins should feel like a gift, not a verdict.
- **Assess and suggest.** The report never hands back a rewritten page or section; rewrites follow `rewrite.md` when asked, and are offered only in SKILL.md's close. End with the close in SKILL.md under "The close". Short, never a paragraph of pitch.

## Before you send (self-check)

- Do all 5 wins clear the bar — impactful, non-obvious, page-specific, distinct, actionable? Would any feel "meh" to a sharp reader? Replace it or cut it (say so) rather than pad.
- Did every "missing X" win survive the strategic-decision check?
- Does every win quote the page verbatim where relevant, and show a concrete fix?
- Is any risky win flagged for A/B testing, and is no win marked risk-free?
- Is the whole thing one page (two max), no walls of text, no fluff, no preamble?
- Does the scorecard math check out (rows consistent with the headline number), and would rerunning after the fixes visibly move it?
- Delivered in chat, not saved unless asked, ending with the close from SKILL.md?

The section doctrines for the page's in-scope sections (Principles + Scoring criteria); `resources/deep-editing.md` and its editing doctrines as needed; `resources/proof-elements.md` and the proof doctrines; `resources/coach-voice.md`.
