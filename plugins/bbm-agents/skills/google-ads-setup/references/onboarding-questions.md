# Onboarding questions

The kickoff interview. Ask one at a time, in plain words, and skip what the brand folder or earlier answers settle. Each question says why it matters so you can explain it if the owner asks, and what to do with the answer.

## The business and the offer

1. **What do you want more of from Google: calls, form leads, booked appointments, or online sales?**
   Sets the conversion and the bidding. One primary outcome per campaign.
2. **Which service or product should we lead with?** If they say "everything", ask which one makes the most money or has the most room to grow.
   Starting narrow concentrates a small budget. Other lines become later phases.
3. **What makes someone pick you over the others?** Proof: years, licences, reviews, guarantees, speed, price.
   Becomes headlines and callouts. Only facts they can stand behind.
4. **Is there an offer?** Free inspection, consultation, quote, discount, first-month deal.
   Offers lift click and conversion rates; the ad and page must match it.

## Customers and area

5. **Where are your customers?** Cities, radius, states, or "we ship nationwide".
   Locations. Serving 3 towns well beats a whole state thinly.
6. **Who is the customer, and what are they searching when they need you?** Ask for the words customers actually use on the phone.
   Seed keywords in buyer language.
7. **Anyone you don't want?** DIY-ers, students, job seekers, commercial vs residential, out-of-area.
   Negatives and exclusions.

## Money

8. **What's an average job or order worth?** And for repeat businesses, what's a customer worth over a year?
9. **Out of 10 leads, how many become customers?**
   8 × 9 = what a lead is worth. Break-even cost per lead = average sale × close rate (use gross profit if they know it). This decides whether the numbers can work.
10. **What monthly budget are you comfortable testing for 3 months?**
    Fewer than ~15 conversions a month means a narrow plan. Three months, because the first month is mostly learning.
11. **How many new leads or orders a week can you actually handle?**
    Caps the budget. Paying for leads nobody calls back is the most common small-business waste.

## Operations

12. **When do calls and forms get answered?** Weekday hours, weekends, after hours.
    Ad schedule. Leads that hit voicemail convert far worse.
13. **Where do leads go today?** Email, a CRM (which one), a spreadsheet, a phone.
    Whether we can feed closed deals back to Google (offline conversions), the biggest lever for lead quality.

## Tracking and history

14. **Is the Google tag or Google Tag Manager on the site? Google Analytics 4? Call tracking?**
    Nothing launches until a test conversion shows up in Google Ads.
15. **Have you run Google Ads before? What happened?** If yes: which account, and may we look at it (run `google-ads-audit`).
    Past search terms and conversions are the best keyword research there is.
16. **What pages can ads send people to?** A page per service is ideal; the homepage is a last resort.
    Landing pages set Quality Score and conversion rate. Missing pages go on the "waiting on you" list (the SEO Desk's `seo-page` skill can draft them).

## Competition and brand

17. **What's your business name, and how do people misspell it?**
    Brand campaign and brand negatives.
18. **Who do you lose jobs to?** Two or three names.
    Competitor research (their ads in the Google Ads Transparency Center, their pages as `--url` seeds). Bidding on competitor names is a later, optional phase.

## When to stop and say "not yet"

- No page on the site can take a lead, and none can be built soon.
- No way to know when a lead or sale came from an ad (no tag, no call tracking, no form notification they can count).
- Break-even cost per lead is below what the keyword bids imply even at a good conversion rate.
- Buyer-intent searches in their area are close to zero.

Say which one, what would fix it, and offer the fix (tracking plan, landing page draft, a different channel).
