# Conversion rules

Evidence tags: [D] demonstrated in the source videos, [R] published research or benchmark, [I] our inference. Treat [I] as a starting point, not a law.

## Universal

- One conversion goal per page. Hero readable in 3 seconds: headline, subhead, CTA, compressed proof, ideally 10 words or fewer in the headline.
- The first screen must visibly continue into the second (no false floor).
- Reading level 5th to 7th grade. Cover objections; don't chase length.
- One primary CTA per section, repeated at decision points; sticky CTA on mobile for long pages.

## Structure by traffic type

| Traffic | Visitor intent | Structure | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid search, urgent ("need X today") | hours | Headline mirrors the query, a dominant call button, form as fallback, named proof, what happens next. No navigation. Run only when someone answers the phone. | under ~300 words [I] |
| Paid search, researching ("how much", "X vs Y") | days | Headline mirrors the query, mechanism subhead, CTA (form-first, one qualifier as step 1), proof, 3-step process, who it's not for, price transparency, FAQ from real queries, CTA again | ~600-1,000 words [R] |
| Paid social (interrupted, colder) | low to mid | Visual and message match with the ad, pain named first, proof early, a lower-commitment first step (quiz, instant quote, "see if you qualify"), objections, CTA | medium [I]: expect it to need more persuasion than search |
| Cold, organic, homepage | mixed | Clear positioning for strangers, paths for "ready" and "researching", navigation allowed | varies; SEO handled by SEO Desk |
| High-ticket own offers ($5k+) | considered | Outcome headline, pain, founder and method, who it's for and not for, case studies with real numbers, offer breakdown, guarantee on process, FAQ, application or booking | long, but 3,000-10,000 word figures come from examples, not tests: a ceiling, not a target |

Message match: one page per intent cluster or ad group once it has real volume, not one page per keyword.

## CTA and form

- CTA copy = verb + what they get ("Book my free inspection"). Never "Submit", "Learn more", "Get started".
- Default fields: name, phone, email. If qualifying matters: step 1 is one tappable question, step 2 is contact details. Multi-step helps at 7+ fields and hurts under ~5.
- Under the button: who calls, when, and the privacy promise ("we never sell your info").
- Consent checkbox (when needed): visible, unchecked, readable. Never tiny grey legal text.
- High-ticket: 5-8 qualifying questions into a calendar is fine.

## Proof

- Named people with photos beat a wall of badges. One third-party rating with its real count is enough.
- Specific, checkable counts beat vague aggregates. Use the brand's approved wording exactly.
- Put testimonials near CTAs. Keep the imperfect sentences; they read as real.
- Regulated niches (legal, insurance, finance, real estate): the results disclaimer sits next to the claim, not in the footer; frame results as comparisons, never predictions.

## Objections

- Cover the four families: price, "will it work for me", trust, why now.
- Write FAQs from real questions (reviews, sales calls, search queries).
- "Who this is not for" is a strong trust device when honesty is the brand's wedge.
- Urgency must be real (real dates, real capacity). No resetting timers.

## Mobile call behaviour (service businesses)

- A thumb-reachable `tel:` button in the hero at 375px, plus a sticky call bar.
- Call-first campaigns only during hours someone answers; missed calls rarely call back [R].
- Mobile LCP 2.5s or less; most local-service traffic is mobile.

## Statistics you must not use in copy

Popular "conversion lift" numbers without a primary source (for example "+332% from scarcity", "social proof +340%", "multi-step forms +206%", "Talk to a human +110%") are vendor marketing. Never put them in client copy or reports as fact.
