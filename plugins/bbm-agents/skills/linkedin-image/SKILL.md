# linkedin-image

One image, one job: make the post's point visible at a glance. If the post has steps or a list of five-plus things, a carousel usually beats one crowded image; suggest `linkedin-carousel` instead.

Helpers: `linkedin.py` (in **linkedin-post** `scripts/`) and `visuals.py` (in **social-visuals** `scripts/`). `python3` on macOS/Linux, `python` or `py` on Windows.

## Before you start

1. `linkedin.py paths [--business <name>]`. Needs `root.css` and `brand-bible.md` (missing → offer `brand-bible`); read `linkedin.json`.
2. Get the post (the draft file, or the owner pastes it). The image supports the post; it never says something the post doesn't.

## Step 1: Pick the kind and route

| Kind | Route | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Framework** (3-6 steps or blocks) | html | The post has a method, steps or a comparison |
| **Big number** | html | One real number carries the post |
| **Quote card** | html | One line from the post is worth screenshotting |
| **Illustration or scene** | image | The post is a story, a feeling, a metaphor |
| **Styled poster** | image | The owner wants something bolder than their usual look |

Say which and why in one line, with the words that will be on it. The owner can switch. Size: 1080x1350 (portrait, biggest in the feed) unless they ask for square (1080x1080) or wide (1200x627).

## Step 2a: HTML (free)

1. `linkedin.py new image --slug "<topic>"` makes the folder.
2. `visuals.py scaffold --kind single --out <folder>/image.html --size <size> --brand-css <root.css>`.
3. Fill it in. Headline 5-8 words. Blocks: as many as the content has, as long as each is readable at phone size (text never under 28px). Footer: author from `linkedin.json`, logo from the brand folder (copy it into the folder). For a big-number or quote card, restyle the one section; keep one `<section class="slide">`.
4. `visuals.py render <folder>/image.html --out <folder> --size <size> --no-pdf` → `slide-01.png`.

## Step 2b: Image model (paid)

1. `visuals.py keys` must show the provider ready (setup: **social-visuals**).
2. Write `prompts.json` (one entry) following `social-visuals/references/image-prompts.md`: the style block from `root.css`, the concrete subject, and any words in quotes with "nothing else written". Keep on-image words to about 8.
3. Dry run, tell the owner "1 image with <model> on your <provider> account, price at <link>", wait for yes, then generate with `--aspect 4:5` (or the size they chose).
4. Offer 2 variations only if they ask; each is another paid image.

## Step 3: Check

`visuals.py inspect`, then look at the image: every word spelled exactly, nothing cut off, readable at about 360 pixels wide, brand colours, no stray text, logos, watermarks or warped hands. Fix and redo. Can't view images → say so and ask the owner to check.

## Output and report

In the work folder: the PNG (or generated image), `image.html` or `prompts.json` + `generation.json`, and `README.md` with which post it goes with and alt text (one plain sentence describing the image, for accessibility; LinkedIn has an "Alt text" button when adding an image).

Tell the owner: the kind, the route, the words on it, the path, and "from linkedin-image".

No em dashes in anything you write for the owner, including README and plan files (use a comma, colon or full stop).

## Limits

Drafts only; never posts. No paid image without the owner's yes. No invented numbers or quotes: a quote card quotes the owner or a named, permitted source. No real people's likenesses or other companies' logos in generated images.
