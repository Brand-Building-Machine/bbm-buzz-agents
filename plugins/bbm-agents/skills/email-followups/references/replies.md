# Answering replies

A reply is the whole point of a follow-up, so it gets the most care and the fastest turnaround. Save each answer as a `reply` draft (one email) in the shared draft format and run the checker like any other draft. The examples below are body text only, for a fictional firm, Northwind Bookkeeping (sender Sam).

## First, every time

1. **Stop the running sequence** for this person. Tell the owner which scheduled touches to cancel.
2. **Read their whole email.** Sort it into one bucket below. If it fits two (a yes with a question), answer the question and then take the yes.
3. **Answer what they actually asked, in the first line.** Not a thank-you paragraph, not a restated pitch.
4. **Shorter than theirs.** A two-line email gets a two-line answer.
5. **Match them.** Their greeting style, their level of formality, their medium (if they texted, the owner texts back).
6. **When the ask is a call:** two specific times in their time zone, then the booking link as the fallback. A bare calendar link feels like homework.
7. **One ask.** If they need to do something, name the one thing.
8. **Don't know the answer** (a price not in `offers.md`, a compliance or licensing question, a promise only the owner can make): don't guess. Draft the reply with `[Client to provide: the answer to X]` and flag it.
9. **Speed.** Same business day. For a hot yes, within the hour if the owner can.

## Yes, interested

Signal: "sounds good", "let's talk", "send me times", "what's next".
Do: confirm, make booking effortless, say what the call covers in one line. If they already picked a time, confirm it and stop there.

```text
Hi Priya,

Great. Does Thursday at 9:00 or Friday at 11:30 work? If not, pick any slot here: [book a time](https://example.com/book)

We'll go through the cleanup plan and the monthly close, about 20 minutes.

Sam
```

## Question

Signal: "do you work with X", "how long does it take", "what does it cost".
Do: answer it straight, in the first sentence. Then one small next step. If the true answer is "it depends", say what it depends on in one line and offer the call to pin it down.

```text
Hi Jordan,

Most cleanups like yours take two to three weeks, depending on how many accounts need catching up.

Happy to give you a firm timeline on a short call. Tuesday at 8:00 or Wednesday at 4:30?

Sam
```

## Objection

Never argue, never discount by reflex, never bad-mouth anyone. Acknowledge it, answer the real concern once, and give them an easy next step.

**Price.** Tie the cost back to the problem in their words. Offer a smaller start only if `offers.md` has one. Ask what they had in mind rather than guessing a lower number.

```text
Hi Dana,

Fair question. The fee covers the monthly close plus the cleanup, so you and Dr. Lee see real numbers every month instead of once a year.

If the full scope is more than you planned, what budget did you have in mind? I can tell you honestly what fits inside it.

Sam
```

**Timing** ("not the right time", "after our busy season"). Ask what changes and when, then offer to hold the plan until then.

**"Need to think about it."** Give them the one thing to think with (the forwardable summary, the answer to the question they are stuck on) and ask what they are weighing. Offer a date to reconnect instead of chasing.

```text
Hi Marcus,

Makes sense. Is it the price, the timing, or whether we're the right fit? If you tell me which, I'll send the one thing that helps you decide.

Sam
```

**"Talking to others."** That's healthy. Offer the questions worth asking any provider, the ones where the owner's real answers are strong. Never mention a competitor by name.

## Not now

Signal: "check back in the spring", "maybe next quarter", "we're mid-move".
Do: thank them, ask for (or confirm) a specific month, and say you'll be in touch then. Suggest a reminder to the owner for that date in their task list or CRM; you don't create it without their yes. They don't go on a marketing list unless they already opted in or say yes to it.

```text
Hi Alex,

Understood. Would early March be a better time to pick this up? I'll get in touch then, and not before.

Sam
```

## No

Signal: "not interested", "we went with someone else", "we'll keep doing it ourselves".
Do: one short, gracious line. No counter-pitch, no "can I ask why" chase. Mark closed-lost with the reason if they gave one. That's the end of the follow-up.

```text
Hi Dana,

Thanks for letting me know, I appreciate the straight answer. If anything changes, you know where to find me.

Sam
```

## Wrong person

Signal: "I don't handle this", "talk to our office manager".
Do: thank them and ask who does, or whether they'd pass it on. Don't add the new person to any sequence without the owner's yes, and mention the referrer by name only if they said that's fine.

```text
Hi Jordan,

Thanks for the pointer. Who handles the books on your side? A name and email is plenty, and I'll mention you sent me.

Sam
```

## Out of office

Signal: an auto-reply with a return date.
Do: **don't reply.** Pause the sequence. The next touch moves to the first weekday morning after they're back, then the usual gaps apply. If the auto-reply names a colleague for urgent matters, a sales follow-up isn't urgent: don't redirect it to them. No return date: wait a week, then resume.

## Unsubscribe or stop

Signal: "unsubscribe", "remove me", "stop emailing me", any clear opt-out.
Do: honor it at once and for good. Tell the owner, the same day, to remove the person from every list, sequence and CRM cadence. One short confirmation is fine; no explanation, no apology essay, no "before you go". Nothing else ever goes to them from these skills.

```text
Done, you won't hear from me again. Sorry for the bother.

Sam
```

## Upset or complaint

Signal: anger, a problem with past work, a public-review threat.
Do: stop selling completely. Draft a short, human acknowledgment for the owner to personalize and suggest they call rather than email. Never argue in writing.
