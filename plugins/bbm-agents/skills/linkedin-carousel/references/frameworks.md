# Carousel structures

A carousel is not a blog post cut into squares. Every slide has two jobs: deliver one idea, and make the next swipe worth it. Pick the structure before writing a single slide.

Adapted from the carousel frameworks in coreyhaines31/marketingskills (MIT) and the carousel brief in charlie947/social-media-skills (MIT).

## Pick one

| The content is... | Structure |
|---|---|
| A list of tips, tools, mistakes or resources | **Stack** |
| A result the owner got, and the system behind it | **Proof** |
| Several named techniques on one theme | **Moves** |
| A strong opinion about a common practice | **Stand** |
| A process a customer goes through, step by step | **Walkthrough** |
| A term or decision customers find confusing | **Explainer** |

## Stack (5-10 slides)

The cover promises an exact count and an exact payoff; every slide pays it down.
1. Cover: "[N] [things] for [who / outcome]". The number is a promise; deliver exactly N.
2. to N-1. One item per slide: the item as the headline, one line on why or how.
Last: one action.
Fails when: filler items pad the count. A tight 6 beats a padded 10.

## Proof (6-10 slides)

Opens with a result stated as fact, closes with the evidence.
1. Cover: the result with a number. "We cut quote turnaround from 5 days to 1."
2. The real problem, named so the reader recognises themselves.
3-4. The system: named steps, named tools.
5 to N-1. The detail worth saving: the checklist, the script, the template.
Last: the proof (a real number, a real before/after the owner can back up) and one action.
Fails when: the middle is vague ("optimise your process"), so the result reads as luck.

## Moves (6-10 slides)

A cover that says most people get this wrong, then numbered, **named** techniques.
1. Cover: a claim or real statistic that most people fail at this.
2. Why the usual approach fails.
3 to N-1. "Move 1: [named technique]", with a before/after or bad/good line.
Last: one line tying the moves together, then one action.
Fails when: two moves are the same idea reworded. Merge them.

## Stand (4-8 slides)

The owner's conviction is the content; the structure keeps it fair.
1. Cover: the position, plainly. "Most benefits renewals are sold, not reviewed."
2 to N-2. Specific evidence, one piece per slide, raising the stakes.
N-1. The fairness line: what they are *not* saying ("This isn't about brokers. It's about the calendar.").
Last: the sign-off, owned by name.
Fails when: the fairness slide is skipped (reads as a rant) or it attacks something the owner visibly does.

## Walkthrough (5-10 slides)

Show the finished outcome first, then the steps.
1. Cover: the outcome. "From a shoebox of receipts to a filed return in 4 steps."
2. The frustration it replaces.
3. The overview: all steps in one list, so readers know how long the road is.
4 to N-1. One step per slide with what happens and what the customer does.
Last: the outcome again and one action.
Fails when: steps are crammed together or the overview slide is missing.

## Explainer (5-8 slides)

For the term or choice customers nod along to but don't understand.
1. Cover: the confusing thing, named. "Deductible vs out-of-pocket max, in 60 seconds."
2. The plain-English definition, one sentence.
3 to N-1. One real example per slide, with numbers the reader can follow.
Last: the one rule of thumb, and one action.

## Rules for every structure

- **Slide 1 is the thumbnail.** It shows alone in the feed before anyone knows there's more. It must stop the scroll by itself: biggest type, fewest words (8 or fewer).
- **One idea per slide.** Headline up to about 8 words, body up to about 20-30. Needs more → split the slide.
- **One visual frame.** Same layout, type sizes and colours on every inside slide; only the content changes.
- **Numbers are real.** Any count on the cover is delivered exactly. Statistics come from the owner or a named, checked source, printed small on the slide.
- **The last slide has one action**, not three: follow, save, book the call, or the real offer from `offers.md`.
- **The post text above the carousel is its own post.** Write it with `linkedin-post` rules (its own first line, not "see attached", not a repeat of slide 1).

## LinkedIn format

- Uploaded as a **PDF document post**. 1080x1350 (4:5) shows biggest in the feed; 1080x1080 is the square option.
- The owner adds a document title when uploading; suggest one (it shows on the post).
- Slides as PNGs are also useful for Instagram or a multi-image post.
