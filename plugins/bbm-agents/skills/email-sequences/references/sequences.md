# Sequence playbooks

The job of every email in each sequence. Adapt the count and order to the business; keep the arc. Timing is counted from the trigger (Day 0). Every email: one job, hook first, one call to action, real facts only.

## Four frameworks you'll use

| Framework | Shape | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem, agitate, reframe** | Name the problem plainly. Show what it costs them (time, money, stress). Reframe how to think about it, without pitching. | Naming the big problem |
| **Before, after, how** | Where the client was. Where they are now, specifically. What made the difference (the method, not the pitch). | Client stories (real ones only) |
| **Old way, new way, what's possible** | How most people handle this and why it keeps them stuck. The shift. What changes after it. | Belief shifts, and the fallback when there is no case study |
| **Value, gap, bridge** | What they've learned so far. What is still hard to do alone. How the offer fills that gap. | The bridge from free to paid |

The reader is the hero; the owner is the guide who has helped people like them. Talk about their outcome, not the owner's features.

## lead-magnet: deliver it (2 emails)

Purpose: keep the promise fast and get the freebie used. **No selling.**

1. **Day 0, immediately: Deliver.** Subject says what it is ("Your water-heater checklist"). Link to it near the top. One or two lines on where to start inside it. What emails come next and how often. End with one question they can answer in a few words that tells the owner who they are (a segmenting question: "Reply and tell me: are you a homeowner or a landlord?"). Replies also help the emails land in the inbox.
2. **Day 1 or 2: Get it used.** The link again. One quick win from inside it, spelled out. Ask them to reply with what they tried or what they're stuck on.

If they answer the segmenting question, the owner can tag them and the welcome sequence can use the matching example or story.

## welcome: introduce, earn trust, bridge (5 to 7 emails)

Purpose: turn a stranger into someone who trusts the owner enough to book a call. Roughly 80 percent useful, 20 percent about the business.

1. **Day 2: Why I do this.** The owner's real story in a few lines: where they started, what they saw go wrong for clients, why they do it this way now. Tell them what's coming.
2. **Day 4: Name their big problem.** Problem, agitate, reframe. Show you understand it better than they could say it themselves.
3. **Day 6: Quick win.** One thing they can do in about 10 minutes, as steps. What they should see after. Ask them to reply when they've tried it.
4. **Day 8: The belief shift.** Old way, new way. The mistake most people in their position make and what to do instead.
5. **Day 10: A client story.** Before, after, how. Real, with the client's permission. No real story yet: write a second belief-shift or a "what I'd do in your shoes" email instead, and mark `[Client to provide: a client story]` in the report.
6. **Day 12 (optional): Objection or myth.** The thing that stops people from getting help ("I can do it myself", "it's too expensive", "I've been burned before"). Answer it honestly.
7. **Day 14 to 16: The bridge.** Value, gap, bridge. Introduce the offer as the next step for people who want it done with them or for them. Low-friction call to action: book a short call, or reply with a word. Then they move into `sales` or a regular newsletter.

## nurture: stay useful until they're ready (6 to 8 emails)

For lists that aren't ready to buy yet (long sales cycles, seasonal needs, cold leads from events). Every 3 to 7 days. Rotate:

- A useful how-to (quick win) tied to a problem the offer solves
- A deep dive on one problem and what it costs to ignore
- The owner's framework or process, explained in plain steps (shows how they think)
- A real client story
- What makes the owner's approach different from the usual options
- A common objection answered
- A direct but friendly offer: "If you want help with this, here's how it works" with one call to action

Each email ends with a small reply ask or a single link. Never two asks.

## sales: make the case for one offer (5 or 6 emails)

Purpose: present a specific offer to people who already know the owner. Every 1 to 2 days. Be direct about what it is and what it costs to do nothing; never pushy.

1. **Introduce the offer.** Their problem, the owner as the guide who's solved it for others, the offer as the path. What it is in a sentence or two, and the outcome. Call to action: see if it's a fit (book a call, reply, or read the details).
2. **Proof.** One real client story, before, after, how. Nothing real yet: use the owner's process and what the first 30 days look like instead.
3. **Objection one.** Usually price or "is it worth it". Acknowledge it, reframe it (cost of the problem versus cost of the fix, using their numbers not invented ones), back it with proof or a guarantee the owner actually offers.
4. **"Will this work for me?"** Who it's for and who it isn't for. Name the situations it fits. Being clear about who it isn't for builds trust.
5. **The shift.** Old way, new way, what's possible. Paint what changes once it's handled.
6. **Close.** Recap in three lines. The real reason to decide now, if there is one: limited onboarding spots this month, a price change date, a cohort start. **No real deadline means no deadline.** "Reply or book here" and the offer stays open.

Service businesses rarely sell straight from email. The call to action is usually a call, an application or a reply; the sale happens in the conversation (then `email-followups`).

## course: a 5-day email course (Day 0 plus 5)

A lead magnet in email form. Builds authority daily; can make a soft offer on Day 5.

- **Day 0:** Welcome. What they'll be able to do by Day 5, the outline, one small thing to do today.
- **Days 1 to 5:** one lesson per day. Each: the lesson, an example, one small action, a teaser for tomorrow. Day 5 adds a soft bridge to the offer.

Pick one format:

| Format | Best for | Each day is |
|---|---|---|
| Crash course | Beginners | Key ideas, then mistakes, steps, upgrades, best practices |
| Step by step | A clear process to an outcome | One step, with one thing to do |
| Biggest mistakes | Avoiding costly errors | One mistake and how to fix it |
| Easy templates | Plug-and-play help | One template and how to use it |

## reengagement: wake up or clean up (3 emails)

For subscribers who haven't clicked or replied in about 90 days. Quiet subscribers drag down delivery for everyone else.

1. **Day 0: Something genuinely useful**, the best thing the owner has, with no ask.
2. **Day 4: "Still want these?"** Plain and honest. One click or a one-word reply keeps them on.
3. **Day 10: The goodbye.** They'll be removed unless they reply or click. Then the owner actually removes them. Say so in the report; the owner does the removing in their platform.

## Measuring

Judge sequences by replies, clicks and calls booked. Open rates are unreliable now (privacy features and inbox previews count opens that never happened), so never promise or report an open rate as proof. Change one thing at a time when testing.
