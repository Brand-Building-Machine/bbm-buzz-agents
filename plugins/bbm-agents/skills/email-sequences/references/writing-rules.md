# Writing rules for every email

The shared quality bar for all four email skills. Read it before drafting; run through the final pass before showing the owner anything.

## The five that matter most

1. **Never invent anything.** No testimonials, client names (not even first names), results, numbers, statistics, dates, guarantees, prices or quotes unless they came from the brand folder, the owner's files, or the owner in this conversation. Missing: ask, or write `[Client to provide: a real client result for this email]`, or restructure the email so it doesn't need the fact. An invented case study sent under the owner's name is a trust problem, not a style problem.
2. **One email, one job.** One idea, one ask. Two ideas means two emails.
3. **Sound like the owner, not like marketing.** Use their voice file. The test: would they say this sentence out loud to a client? If not, rewrite it.
4. **Every email stands on its own.** A reader who never opened the last one must still get it. Follow-ups bring something new (an angle, a result, a useful thing, a clean close), never "just checking in."
5. **Drafts only.** Nothing is sent, scheduled, loaded into an email platform or added to a list without the owner's explicit yes for that specific batch.

## Structure

- **Hook first.** The first line earns the second. Start with them, their problem, or the thing they asked for. Not with you, and not with "I hope this finds you well."
- **Short paragraphs.** One to three sentences. Most people read on a phone.
- **Plain words.** Aim for a reading level a 12-year-old handles easily. Cut jargon, or translate it the first time.
- **More "you" than "I/we".**
- **One call to action.** Name it plainly ("Book a 15-minute call", "Reply with 1, 2 or 3"). A second link is fine only if it is the same action.
- **Low-friction asks beat big ones** early in a relationship. "Worth a look?" before "Book a 60-minute strategy session."
- **Sign off as a person**, with the owner's own sign-off.
- **Plain text by default.** Service businesses sell trust; a personal-looking email beats a designed template for anything one-to-one (follow-ups, onboarding, sales). Designed layouts are for newsletters and announcements, and only when the owner uses them.

## Subject lines and preview text

- Clear beats clever. The subject says what the email is about, or opens a genuine question.
- Under about 50 characters; the checker warns over 60.
- No ALL CAPS, no exclamation marks, no emoji unless the owner's voice uses them.
- Never fake a reply or forward (`Re:` / `Fwd:` on a first email). It is deceptive and gets people to mark it as spam.
- Preview text finishes the thought the subject started. It never repeats the subject.

## Banned phrases (the checker rejects these)

"I hope this email finds you well", "I hope you're doing well", "just checking in", "just following up", "just circling back", "circling back", "touching base", "I wanted to reach out", "bumping this to the top of your inbox", "did you get a chance to", "per my last email", "as per my previous email", "I never heard back", "don't hesitate to reach out", "please do not hesitate", "at your earliest convenience", "I'd love to pick your brain", "game-changer", "unlock your potential", "take it to the next level", "in today's fast-paced world", "delve", "synergy", "leverage" (as a verb).

Also avoid: opening with a compliment about their business, fake urgency (a deadline that isn't real), guilt ("I guess you're not interested"), and passive-aggressive nudges.

## Punctuation

- **No em dashes or en dashes.** Use a comma, a full stop, a colon or brackets. (Hyphens in compound words are fine.)
- No exclamation-mark stacking. One per email at most, and usually none.

## The final pass (do it every time)

Before you show a draft:

1. **Fact check against sources.** Every number, name, result and claim traces to the brand folder or the owner. If not, remove it or mark it `[Client to provide]`.
2. **Read it as the recipient** on a phone, busy. Is the point clear in the first two lines? Can they answer the ask in five words or fewer?
3. **The AI-tell sweep.** Cut: tidy triplets ("fast, simple, and effective"), "not just X, but Y", rhetorical questions stacked back to back, every paragraph the same length, generic empathy ("I know how hard it can be"), throat-clearing openers, summarizing endings ("In short..."). Vary sentence length. Keep one genuine, specific opinion the owner actually holds.
4. **Cut 20 percent.** Then read it again. Shorter almost always wins.
5. **Run the checker:** `emailkit.py check <file>` must exit 0. Read every warning; fix or justify it.
