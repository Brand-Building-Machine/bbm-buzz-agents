# Hooks and mechanics

Adapted from Motion's hook-writing and creative-mechanics skills (MIT) and the hook system in coreyhaines31/marketingskills (MIT).

## A hook is three things (video) or two (static)

- **Video:** the first frame or action, the first spoken line, and the on-screen text. They should work together and **not repeat each other**: if the voice says it, the text adds to it. The spoken line must still work with the sound off, because the text carries it.
- **Static:** the words on the image and the first line of primary text. Same rule: the image headline and the first line do different jobs.

The hook's job is to make the right people stop and the wrong people keep scrolling. On Meta that second half matters: the people who stop tell the system who to find next.

## Eight triggers

Every hook uses at least one. Vary them across a hook set.

1. **Pattern interrupt:** unexpected or counterintuitive. "Stop shopping your health plan every year."
2. **Identity call-out:** the right people self-select. "If you run a company with 10-50 staff, this is for you." Talk to the role or situation, never assert the viewer's personal attributes (health, finances, age, religion and so on). Meta rejects those.
3. **Pain agitation:** mirror the inner monologue. "The renewal letter came in 30% higher. Again."
4. **Curiosity gap:** an open loop. "The one question to ask your broker before you sign."
5. **Social proof:** real, specific, sourced. "Last year we moved 40 local companies to better plans." Only with the owner's numbers.
6. **Contrarian:** challenge a belief. "Switching brokers takes one signature, not a month."
7. **Aspiration:** the other side. "Imagine renewal season taking one call."
8. **Stakes:** the cost of doing nothing. "Two weeks until renewal. Here's what to check."

## Hook standards

Do: write in the customer's voice; use specific numbers, times and details (the owner's, never invented); lead with the pain or desire, not the business name; make it feel written for one person; read it out loud.

Don't: open with "Introducing", "Discover", "Are you looking for"; open with the brand name; write a hook any competitor could run unchanged; use corporate language; repeat one trigger across the whole set.

## Mechanics (the idea's architecture)

| Mechanic | How it works | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Implied answer** | Hook asks a question that sounds like an observation; the visual silently answers it | Unaware, problem-aware |
| **Social witness** | Someone else reacts to the result ("Wait, your renewal went *down*?") | Problem- and solution-aware |
| **Overheard conversation** | Looks like a real exchange: two owners talking, a text thread, a comment reply | Lowering ad resistance |
| **Reframe** | Flips a belief. "It's not a price problem. It's a timing problem" | Solution-aware |
| **Borrowed enemy** | The old way or the typical competitor, unnamed. "The broker who emails a PDF once a year" | Solution- and product-aware |
| **Trojan horse** | Useful content first (a checklist, a tip), the offer after | Unaware, problem-aware |
| **Contrast without comment** | Two things side by side, no verdict. Viewer concludes | Any stage |
| **This and a...** | Pairs the service with something the viewer already wants | Desire-led |

One primary mechanic per concept; a second can support it.

## Iterating on a winner

When meta-audit marks an ad a winner and it starts to tire, change one thing at a time, in this order of impact: 1) the hook (changes who stops), 2) the visual treatment, 3) the format, 4) body copy and call to action. Launch the new version **beside** the winner; never edit the winner.

After a delivery kill (Meta barely spent on it), change the hook or visual; the copy never got read. After a quality kill (wrong people), change the angle and add "who this is for". After a cost kill, look at the offer.

## Identity keywords

A simple, strong lever for service businesses: take a working ad and make variants that name one niche in the hook or headline ("...for **dental practices**", "...for **restaurants**"). The word lets the right people self-select and gives Meta a clear signal of who the ad is for. Keep everything else the same so the test is clean.
