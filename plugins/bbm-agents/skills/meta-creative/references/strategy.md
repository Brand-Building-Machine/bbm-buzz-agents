# Creative strategy: from customer words to angles

Adapted from Motion's creative-strategy skills (review-audit, creative-strategy-engine; MIT) by Alysha, Creative Strategy Lead at Motion. Rewritten for service businesses running lead ads.

## 1. Customer language

### From reviews
1. **Group by service** if the business sells several things. Everything below runs per service.
2. **Score each review 1-5:**
   - 1: no signal ("great service!", "5 stars"). Discard.
   - 2: short and vague.
   - 3: mentions the service with some detail, no emotion.
   - 4: a real experience, a before/after, or a feeling.
   - 5: gold. Long, specific, emotional. The customer wrote an essay.
3. **Pull quotes only from 4s and 5s**, word for word, never tidied up. 2s and 3s can support a pattern.
4. **Five buckets**, with a one-line pattern summary and the best 2-4 quotes each:
   - **Pain points:** what was wrong before, in their words.
   - **Trigger moments:** what finally made them call ("renewal letter came in 30% higher").
   - **Objections before buying:** what nearly stopped them ("thought switching would be a nightmare").
   - **Transformations:** the before → after.
   - **Ad-ready phrases:** lines that could sit in an ad unchanged.

### No reviews: ask the owner
1. What's going on in someone's life the week they call you?
2. What do they say on the first call, in their words?
3. What almost stops people from signing?
4. What do happy customers say after?
5. Who calls you who is *not* a fit, and how can you tell early?

Question 5 feeds the "who this is not for" line that protects lead quality.

## 2. Anchor: pain or desire

Default to **pain**. Nearly every service business solves a problem. Desire-first is for aspirational purchases (luxury, status, aesthetics). A demographic alone is never the message:

- Weak: "Are you a small business owner? Talk to us about benefits."
- Strong: "Your renewal came in 30% higher and your broker sent a PDF. That's not advice."

Pain plus context tells people why it matters *to them*.

## 3. Personas

2-4 people who feel the **same pain in different lives**. One pain maps to several personas and one persona can have several pains; every intersection is a possible angle.

Define each by life context and, most importantly, **how they experience this pain differently**. For lead gen, only include personas who can become qualified leads (match `qualified_lead_means`). A persona who will never buy is an expensive persona.

Example: rising health-insurance costs →
- Owner of a 12-person company: the renewal letter lands and they have two weeks to decide; they feel they're overpaying but don't know what to ask.
- Office manager who inherited benefits: they're blamed for the increase and have no one to call.
- Founder hiring their first employees: benefits feel like a maze and a trap.

## 4. Angles

An angle is the core truth for one pain x persona, said the way a person would say it. Not a tagline.

| Pain | Persona | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Rising premiums | 12-person company owner | "Your broker only calls at renewal" |
| Inherited benefits admin | Office manager | "You didn't sign up to be the benefits department" |
| Roof leak after storm | Homeowner | "Insurance will cover more than the first roofer told you" |

For each angle, note: the use case (how this person hits the pain), the deepest desire, which features matter most to them *as benefits*, and their top objection. The objection becomes proof or reassurance in the copy.

Test: would a real customer say this sentence out loud? If it sounds like marketing, it isn't the angle yet.

## 5. Awareness stage

| Stage | They know... | Lead the ad with |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | nothing is wrong | a story or observation that reveals the problem |
| Problem-aware | the pain, not the fix | the pain, in their words |
| Solution-aware | fixes exist, not yours | why your way is different |
| Product-aware | you, not yet convinced | proof, reviews, guarantee, the offer |
| Most-aware | you, ready | the offer and the next step |

Cold broad audiences for local lead gen are mostly **problem-aware**. Retargeting (site visitors, past leads, video viewers) is **product-aware** or later. Match the hook to the stage; a product-aware hook shown to cold traffic reads as noise.

## 6. The creative matrix

Pain x persona x angle x stage x mechanic x format. You're not writing random ads; you're covering this map one deliberate cell at a time. Track which cells were tested (angle, mechanic and format in the concept name) so the next batch fills gaps instead of repeating.
