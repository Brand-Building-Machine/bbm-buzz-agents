# Sourcing and fact-check rules

## Source tiers

| Tier | What | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Primary: government and regulators (.gov), official product or program docs, laws, standards bodies, primary datasets, peer-reviewed research | Preferred for every fact |
| 2 | Named studies with a stated method, original industry research, established nonprofits' surveys | Fine; cite the study itself, not a blog about it |
| 3 | Reputable journalism or trade press that links to its source | Only when no primary source exists; better to follow the link and cite the upstream |
| 4 | Generic marketing blogs, affiliate "best of" roundups, unsourced explainers | Never |
| 5 | Content farms, scraped or AI-spun pages | Never |

**Echo rule**: ten articles repeating one study are one source. Find and cite the original.

Examples for employee benefits and insurance: Tier 1 healthcare.gov, CMS, IRS (HSA/FSA and other annual limits), Department of Labor / EBSA (ERISA, COBRA), the state insurance department, BLS. Tier 1-2: KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey. Tier 3: SHRM, trade press that links upstream.

## Citing

- Inline link on the claim: `<Claim with the number> ([Publisher, Year](https://exact-page-url)).` Year in the citation when the number changes over time.
- Record for each figure: publisher, page title, URL, the date or period it covers.
- Laws, deadlines, limits, eligibility rules and "best/first/only" claims are load-bearing even without numbers: source them.
- Annual limits change every year. A figure from last year is wrong for this year's post.

## Verification scores

| Score | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | Exact value on the cited page, in matching context | keep |
| 0.7-0.9 | Fair paraphrase ("43%" written as "over 40%") | keep |
| 0.3-0.6 | Page is on topic but the figure isn't visible; or a homepage cited for a specific figure | find the exact page or cut |
| 0 | Not on the page, or contradicted, or a Tier 4-5 source | fix or cut |
| unverified | No link | source or cut |

A stale-year mismatch scores no higher than 0.5.

## Never

- Invent a statistic, a study, a quote, an expert, a client, or a result.
- Soften an unverifiable number into "many" or "most" to keep it. Cut it.
- Cite a source for something it doesn't say.
- Write "studies show" without the study.
